# Draw Steel Design Principles

> The genre frame and craft principles that color every artifact this agent produces. Loaded at every context-load alongside the active skill's references. When a craft choice could go multiple ways, the principle that aligns with these is the right one.

## What Draw Steel is — and isn't

Draw Steel is **heroic fantasy with tactical, cinematic combat**. Other genres can be touched — comedy, horror, downtime, intrigue — but never centered. When designing content, default toward the heroic-fantasy frame; lean away from genre conventions Draw Steel explicitly disclaims.

**Draw Steel is not:**

- A dungeon-crawl. "Clear the rooms" is not a valid encounter objective; combat happens *in* dungeons but isn't *about* dungeons.
- A hex-crawl or wilderness-survival game. Exploration encounters should not default to weather, navigation tests, getting lost, or wilderness travel as the primary obstacle.
- A horror RPG. Horror as flavor is fine; sustained horror tone belongs in a different game.
- A comedy RPG. Comedy as moment is fine; sustained slapstick belongs elsewhere.
- An attrition wargame. Every fight should hold narrative weight — see the [combat-encounter](${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/combat-encounter/SKILL.md) skill's craft principles for the full enforcement.

When a user request drifts toward one of these disclaimed genres, surface the option to reshape it back to Draw Steel's frame — or honor their explicit choice and note the deviation in the artifact.

## Tactical

Draw Steel rewards **positioning and teamwork**, not raw force.

- **Combat happens on a grid.** Distances, areas, and movement are measured in squares. Encounters call out positioning — terrain features, sight lines, choke points, range bands — rather than abstracting into "the heroes face the enemy."
- **Teamwork is the assumed mode.** The best fights reward coordination: combos, setup-and-payoff turns, characters covering each other's weaknesses. When framing a fight, ask: *what's the teamwork moment this scene wants to enable?*
- **Players have many choices each round.** Never write a fight that reduces to "swing your sword." If the encounter only rewards one tactic, the encounter is mis-shaped.

Draw Steel is not a wargame — but it is tactical. Output should reflect that.

## Heroic

Heroic operates at two layers: **stance** and **scope**.

### Stance (default character framing)

Default to PCs as **protagonists with positive motivations**. They might be reluctant. They might have ambiguous backstories. But the baseline assumption is that they will do the right thing — even when it's hard, even when greed or self-interest pulls the other way.

When framing PC hooks, NPC stakes, and adventure premises, lead with the heroic posture. If the user explicitly signals a darker tone (mercenary campaign, morally ambiguous setting), follow their lead — but don't default there.

### Scope (onscreen vs offscreen)

If a hero in a movie, comic, or novel wouldn't be shown doing it, Draw Steel doesn't track it. **Don't include in scenes or as mechanical obstacles:**

- Encumbrance or item weight
- Rations / food consumption
- Light sources / torch tracking *(except where light is a deliberate tactical feature of the scene)*
- Ammunition counts
- Other "tedious bookkeeping" that wouldn't appear in fiction about these heroes

This is enforceable, not aspirational. If an exploration encounter draft says "the party loses 2 rations per day," that's wrong content for this game — rewrite.

## Cinematic

Draw Steel content should help the reader **see the scene**. This is a writing principle that applies to every artifact, not just encounters.

- **Names should be evocative, not descriptive.** A shadow's escape ability is *In All This Confusion*, not *Disengage*. A trap is *The Floor Falls Away*, not *Pit Trap*. A negotiation beat is *The Duke Plays His Last Card*, not *NPC reveals secret*. Naming carries weight — invest in it.
- **Action descriptions should imply the camera.** Encounter introductions, NPC entrances, and outcome summaries should read like scene direction. The reader should be able to picture the moment.
- **Pacing favors the dramatic beat.** When choosing between two equally valid options, prefer the one that lands a clearer moment.

Cinematic is the keyword that distinguishes competent writing from writing that helps a Director run a scene. Apply liberally.

## Magic and psionics are peers

Draw Steel treats psionics — telekinesis, telepathy, mind manipulation — as a **peer tradition to magic**, not a subset of it. The Talent class, the Synlirii, and any psionic NPC should be framed with the same gravity and respect as wizards, conduits, and divine power. Don't describe psionics as "a kind of magic"; they're a parallel tradition.
