# Draw Steel Setting Overview

> High-level summary of the published Draw Steel setting — the timescape, Orden, the world's defining tensions, the broad shape of civilization. Per-region, per-faction, cosmological, and historical detail lives in the sibling files in this folder.

## Orden and the timescape

Draw Steel is set across **the timescape** — a multiverse of worlds spanning high fantasy, dark fantasy, and space fantasy. Each star in Orden's night sky is another world. Old-fashioned people call them "planes"; sages call them "manifolds." They mean the same thing.

The default game world is **Orden**, the prime manifold — a place where humans, elves, dwarves, and orcs share the world with dragons, goblins, kobolds, and dozens of other speaking peoples. Human civilization and politics dominate. Orden contains eight major regions, the largest of which is Vasloria.

The current era is the **Age of Chaos**.

## Genre register

Orden is a **heroic-fantasy world**, not a high-fantasy one — even though there are some pretty high-fantasy things going on in it. The average person has very little access to magic. A typical village might have a priest who knows minor healing prayers, or an alchemist or hedge wizard who can brew potions, but that's the extent of it.

Technology and society sit at roughly **13th-century-Europe** levels, but quality of life is closer to ours: people live about as long as we do, die from disease about as often as we do, and eat similarly varied (if more constrained) diets. Magic, prayer, and psionics mean Orden never industrializes — science as we know it likely never arrives.

In Capital, flying tapestries shrink the city to minutes-across — but only the very wealthy can afford them. The vast majority of citizens live a life basically the same as Shakespeare-era London, with less plague and fire.

10th-level heroes exist, but are exceedingly rare. Most people in Orden have no class levels at all. Only a few are 1st-level anything.

## In-world point of view

All setting facts in this library are presented from the perspective of someone living in Orden. Even learned historians disagree, and new information constantly challenges accepted wisdom. There is no objective certainty.

This is **a usable principle for skills**: when generating setting content, prefer "many people say…" framings to absolute claims. The Dragon Phalanx, for instance, are still widely called "incorruptible" — in spite of concrete evidence to the contrary. That's not a contradiction to clean up; it's the world working as designed.

## Use what you want

The timescape is one possible setting. Directors may use as many or as few of these details as they like, create their own world within the timescape, or ignore the timescape entirely and run their own setting. There are no rules when it comes to worldbuilding — change anything that doesn't suit the campaign. Don't like dwarves with literal stone skin? Make them fleshy, or barrel-chested punks, or whatever else fits. Players should ask their Director what's in scope before building heroes.
