> Orden contains eight major regions. Only those covered in the source material populated so far appear below.

## Vasloria

A forested, medieval, feudal land — the largest region of Orden, peppered with few cities and mostly towns and villages. As of the Age of Chaos, Vasloria's "nations" are not bounded states but geographic areas with names people use to distinguish lands of similar terrain and subculture. Vasloria boasts the densest collection of elf-haunted **wodes** in Orden.

### Aendrim

Land of hills and farms. Most of Aendrim now sits within Omund's Land.

### Corwell

Land of knights and castles. Most of Corwell now sits within Omund's Land.

### Tull

Marshlands where witches and wise women battle hags and swamp monsters.

### Farrow

Thick forests; bands of archers.

### Graid

The horselands; home to the best cavalry in Orden. Parts of Graid sit within Omund's Land.

### Sednia, Olvaria, and Sărda

Small earldoms in mountainous northeastern Vasloria.

### Rhöl

Northeastern earldom containing **Glauer**, once ruled by a deathless count.

### The Great Wode

Vasloria's northern border. The world here still works as it did before humans arrived — before Ord placed the dwarves in the world and imposed the Law of Time on Orden.

### Wodes (general)

Wodes appear across all regions of Orden, but Vasloria's collection is the densest. Within a wode, time misbehaves: cause and effect are only distant cousins. Children's tales of villagers wandering into a wode and emerging unchanged 100 years later are based on real events. When pressed on how this works, the elves look baffled. *"How does what work?"*

## Omund's Land

Western Vasloria — most of Aendrim and Corwell, parts of Graid. Until 15 years ago, this region was ruled by **Good King Omund** and his draconian knights, the Dragon Phalanx (see [factions.md](factions.md)). Roads were safe. People could walk into the woods unafraid of meeting anything more threatening than a nymph or conversational manticore.

Omund was killed and his castle taken by **Ajax the Invincible**, now called the Iron Saint. The forest now claims the towns and roads once held safe; the woods are dangerous. Ajax rules from the **Chrysopolis**, a sky-fortress raised by his wizard Mortum from the secrets of the ancient sky elf flying cities. Faiths and temples are abolished; the dukes who organized the barons have been executed; the far-flung baronies barely hold human civilization together.

Once, there was trade between humans, elves, dwarves, and orcs. Now there is only suspicion.

## Capital

The Greatest City in This or Any Age — *the City of the Great Game*. Located across the Bale Sea from Vasloria, on the eastern coast of **Rioja**. Capital is the largest city in Orden — larger than the fabled steel dwarf capital of Kalas Valiar, larger even than Alloy, the City at the Center of the Timescape (see [cosmology.md](cosmology.md)).

Capital is a city of playwrights and opera, of spies and sorcery. Famed worldwide as a city of high magic where flying tapestries act as taxis, the reality of living in Capital is more mundane — only the very wealthy can afford such luxuries. The vast majority of citizens live a life basically the same as Shakespeare-era London.

The "great game" of Capital is **espionage**. Four ancient great houses share power uneasily with three upstart guilds recently elevated to great-house status (see [factions.md](factions.md)). Three years ago the prince died with no heir, the circumstances unclear. A succession war is brewing — everyone knows fighting on the streets is coming, but each player would prefer someone else make the first move.

## Khemhara

Ancient region; once the seat of the Infinite Pharaoh **Khorsekef**, who ruled from the **Heliopolis**. Khorsekef is now the Ultralich, ruling the Necropolitan Ruin in the Abyssal Waste (see [cosmology.md](cosmology.md)) and intending to return to Orden and reclaim his throne.

## Other regions

Source material to date covers Vasloria, Capital (in Rioja), and Khemhara in detail. Higara, Khoursir, Vanigar, and the remaining major regions are referenced elsewhere in this library but not yet detailed here.
