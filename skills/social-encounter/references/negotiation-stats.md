# Draw Steel — Negotiation Stats

*Source: Draw Steel Heroes, Chapter 11: Negotiation, book p. 284.*

During a negotiation, the Director assigns the NPC four temporary statistics and features:

- **Interest**
- **Patience**
- **Motivations**
- **Pitfalls**

The heroes strike a favorable deal by maximizing the NPC's **interest** through arguments that invoke the NPC's **motivations** and avoid their **pitfalls** — but they have to do all that before the NPC's **patience** wears out.

## Interest

Interest represents how eager the NPC is to make a deal with the heroes. It is graded on a scale of **0 to 5**:

- **0** — no interest at all
- **5** — most possible interest

When a negotiation begins, the NPC's interest is somewhere between **1 and 4**.

- If interest reaches **5**, the NPC makes a final offer and the negotiation ends.
- If interest drops to **0**, the NPC ends the negotiation without offering the heroes any deal.

Interest goes **up or down** during the negotiation based on the arguments the heroes make.

## Patience

Patience represents how much time and effort the NPC is willing to devote to the negotiation. It is graded on a scale of **0 to 5**, with the NPC starting above 0.

- If patience reaches **0**, the NPC makes a final offer and the negotiation ends.

Patience can decrease each time the heroes make an argument.

### Language and Patience

If **one or more** heroes negotiating with the NPC can communicate in the NPC's native language (excluding Caelian), the NPC's patience increases by **+1** at the start of the negotiation (max 5).

If **three or more** heroes can communicate in the NPC's native language, the NPC's patience instead increases by **+2** at the start (max 5).

(See Chapter 4: Background for in-game languages.)

## Motivations

Each NPC has **at least two** motivations the heroes can appeal to.

- Arguments that **appeal to a motivation** require an *easier* power roll to increase the NPC's interest.
- Arguments that don't appeal to any motivation require a *more difficult* power roll.
- See **Making Arguments** for the actual roll mechanics.

Each motivation can be **successfully appealed to only once** during a negotiation. To successfully appeal to a motivation, the heroes must:

- Use the motivation in their argument, **and**
- Not mention one of the NPC's pitfalls, **and**
- Not be caught in a lie.

For the full list of motivations, see [negotiation-motivations-and-pitfalls.md](negotiation-motivations-and-pitfalls.md).

## Pitfalls

Pitfalls are motivations that spark **ire, discomfort, shame, fear, or some other negative response** in the NPC. Using a pitfall in an argument causes both the NPC's interest **and** patience to wane.

- Each NPC has **at least one** pitfall.
- Many NPCs have **at least two**.

Motivations and pitfalls are two sides of the same concept — what is a motivation for one NPC is a pitfall for another. They share a single list. Whenever the heroes argue, they risk stumbling into a pitfall unless they have done their research beforehand or read the NPC well during the scene.

For the full list, see [negotiation-motivations-and-pitfalls.md](negotiation-motivations-and-pitfalls.md).

## Starting Stats by Attitude

The NPC's starting Interest and Patience depend on their attitude toward the heroes. The Director can adjust these as fits the situation — an irascible NPC might have lower patience; a hostile NPC with deep stake in the topic might have higher-than-typical interest.

| Attitude    | Description                                                                                                              | Interest | Patience |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------:|:--------:|
| Hostile     | Openly opposed to the heroes. Barely willing to listen.                                                                  | 1        | 2        |
| Suspicious  | Doubts the heroes' motives but is willing to listen.                                                                     | 2        | 3        |
| Neutral     | Doesn't feel one way or the other. Would probably rather be somewhere else, but doesn't want to be rude.                 | 2        | 3        |
| Open        | Willing to listen, willing to help, as long as the heroes aren't asking too much.                                        | 3        | 4        |
| Friendly    | The heroes seem like the NPC's people. The NPC is willing to give them the benefit of the doubt.                         | 3        | 4        |
| Trusting    | The NPC has reason to take the heroes at their word and will help if the characters don't screw this up.                 | 3        | 5        |

## NPCs Change Over Time

NPCs are complex individuals who can change between negotiations. Heroes might negotiate with the same NPC for several different favors during a campaign, and the NPC's motivations and pitfalls can shift between scenes. Example: the heroes turn a bandit captain who had **greed** and **power** motivations into a temporary ally. The captain learns from them and changes their ways — robbing only those who exploit the poor, giving the takings to people in need. Next time the heroes negotiate with that captain, their motivations are **benevolence** and **protection** instead.
