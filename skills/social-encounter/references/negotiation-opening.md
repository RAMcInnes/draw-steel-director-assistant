# Draw Steel — Opening a Negotiation

*Source: Draw Steel Heroes, Chapter 11: Negotiation, book p. 287.*

A negotiation begins when the heroes ask something of an NPC and the Director deems that the circumstances require a negotiation. Those circumstances always involve:

- The heroes requiring assistance that could **change the course of the adventure**, and
- The NPC being **conflicted** about working with them.

Unlike combat — which can be thrust on the heroes by violent foes or unexpected events — heroes must **willingly** start a negotiation. Characters must want something from an NPC; otherwise there is no reason to negotiate.

The Director can decide that an NPC who has something the players want shows up at their door and asks if the heroes wish to negotiate. But it is always okay for the characters to say *"Not interested,"* and refuse.

## Stop Combat, Start Negotiation

If a hero wants to halt hostilities to negotiate with the other side, they can use a **maneuver** to make a **hard Presence test** (or another applicable test, as the Director determines) to stop combat and start a negotiation.

The test only has a chance of success if the Director believes the other side is **willing and capable** of negotiating. A foe with the upper hand, who hates the heroes beyond measure, or who lacks sapience is unlikely to negotiate.

## Starting Stats

An NPC's starting negotiation stats depend on their **attitude** toward the heroes (see the Negotiation Starting Attitudes table in [negotiation-stats.md](negotiation-stats.md)). The Director can adjust as fits the situation:

- A naturally irascible NPC might have lower patience.
- A hostile NPC with a greater-than-expected stake in the topic might have higher-than-typical interest.

## Uncovering Motivations

If a hero wishes to figure out an NPC's motivations, they can **simply ask** — *"What do you want out of this deal?"* In response, the NPC can willingly hint at or reveal one of their motivations, usually by asking for something. Example: a monarch with the **greed** motivation and a penchant for collecting rare animals might suggest the heroes retrieving a griffon egg would earn his gratitude.

The Director can also decide that **during the natural course of the negotiation** the NPC offers similar suggestions without the heroes asking — provided the NPC's interest is already **3 or higher**.

### Test to Read the NPC

If the NPC isn't forthcoming, or if the heroes want to learn one of the NPC's **pitfalls**, a hero can make a **Reason, Intuition, or Presence** test while interacting with the NPC during the negotiation. Which characteristic depends on the tactics used to draw the NPC out. Outcomes:

> **Power Roll + Reason, Intuition, or Presence:**
>
> - **Tier 1 (low):** The hero learns no information regarding the NPC's motivations or pitfalls, **and** the NPC realizes the hero is trying to read them and becomes annoyed. As a consequence, the NPC's patience is reduced by 1.
> - **Tier 2 (mid):** The hero learns no information regarding the NPC's motivations or pitfalls.
> - **Tier 3 (high):** The hero learns one of the NPC's motivations or pitfalls (the hero's choice).

After this test is made, the heroes can't make another test to determine the **same** NPC's motivations or pitfalls until they make an argument to the NPC or the negotiation ends.

## Outside of Negotiation

While heroes can discover an NPC's motivations or pitfalls through tests during the negotiation, they can also use **other methods of investigation before the scene begins**:

- Research
- Reconnaissance — for instance, reading the NPC's diary, talking to their closest friends, asking around about their reputation

A little legwork before sitting at the table can reveal quite a lot about a person.
