# Draw Steel — NPC Response and Offer

*Source: Draw Steel Heroes, Chapter 11: Negotiation, book p. 289.*

After a hero makes an argument, the NPC responds in **one of three ways**:

- **Positive** — if the heroes increased the NPC's interest. *"That's an excellent point." "You've given me much to consider." "Fair enough." "Makes sense to me."*
- **Negative** — if the heroes decreased the NPC's interest. *"I don't buy that." "Poppycock!" "I hear you, but I disagree." "That's not going to sway me."*
- **Impatient** — if the argument failed to change interest in either direction. *"I've heard that before." "Are you going to offer me anything real?" "This debate is tiresome." "BORING!"*

Unless the NPC is deceitful, the heroes should be able to **clearly tell** whether their argument helped, missed, or backfired.

The initial response should come **with an offer** (or a refusal to make an offer) based on the NPC's **current interest**. If a hero's argument reduces the NPC's patience to **0**, the NPC lets the heroes know that this is their **final offer**.

## Offers by Interest Level

### Interest 5 — *"Yes, and ..."*

The NPC offers everything the heroes initially asked for **and sweetens the deal**. This is the best possible outcome:

- If the heroes offered services or payments, the NPC might **waive those obligations** entirely — the heroes get what they want for free.
- Alternatively, the NPC holds the heroes to their offer **and adds an extra service or item** on top.

**Example:** the heroes asked the boss of a thieves' guild for help against Lord Saxton. At interest 5 the guildmaster pledges a unit of elite assassins **and** offers the heroes a quiver of explosive arrows for the fight.

The NPC should let the heroes know that this is the best offer they can make.

---

### Interest 4 — *"Yes."*

The NPC offers the heroes **everything they asked for** but won't sweeten the deal. The NPC also accepts anything the heroes offered as part of the deal.

**Example:** the heroes offered a jailbreak in exchange for the elite assassins; at interest 4 the guildmaster agrees to those exact terms with no adjustment.

This **likely ends the negotiation** — but the heroes can push for more if the NPC has patience left. A Director can prompt this by having the NPC ask, *"Is there anything else?"* or *"What else do you want from me?"*

---

### Interest 3 — *"Yes, but ..."*

The NPC offers the heroes what they want **in exchange for everything the heroes offered, plus a little extra** — a favor, an additional payment, etc.

**Example:** the heroes offered to free a thieves' guild member from prison in exchange for the organization's assassins. At interest 3 the guildmaster agrees, but asks the heroes to **also** free an additional prisoner, or to grant the rescued prisoner a sum of cash or a magic weapon.

---

### Interest 2 — *"No, but ..."*

The NPC **can't** give the heroes what they want — but is willing to offer something **less impactful** in exchange for what the heroes promised.

**Example:** the guildmaster won't spare any troops to fight Lord Saxton, but offers the **latest spy reports on Saxton's movements** in exchange for the jailbreak.

---

### Interest 1 — *"No."*

The NPC outright **rejects** the heroes' idea without a counteroffer. If they still have patience, they might press for a better deal: *"Why should we risk our necks to help you fight Lord Saxton? What's really in it for the thieves' guild, other than a short, brutal end when you inevitably fail?"*

---

### Interest 0 — *"No, and ..."*

The NPC offers nothing, refuses to negotiate further, **and seeks to harm the heroes**. They might:

- Attack immediately, or
- Take a different approach — spreading malicious rumors, sending assassins, or otherwise making the heroes' lives difficult.

If the heroes don't want to be at odds with the NPC, they will need to offer a **valuable gift or undertake a quest** just to make amends.

> **It is impossible to continue a negotiation when the NPC's interest drops to 0.**

---

## Keep Going or Stop

If the NPC still has patience after making an offer **and** their interest is between **1 and 4**, the heroes can:

- Make **another argument** to try to improve the deal, **or**
- **Accept the offer** and end the negotiation.

Let the players drive the decision. The Director can show the NPC has patience left by having them ask, *"Is there anything else?"*

### When the Negotiation Ends

- If the NPC's **patience is 0** or their **interest is 5**, the offer the NPC makes is their **final offer**. Heroes can accept or not — either way, the negotiation ends.
- If the NPC's **interest is 0**, the negotiation ends without any deal.
- The heroes can **walk away** from a negotiation without accepting a deal at any time.
