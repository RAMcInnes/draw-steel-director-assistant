# Draw Steel — List of Motivations and Pitfalls

*Source: Draw Steel Heroes, Chapter 11: Negotiation, book pp. 284–287.*

An NPC can have any of the following **twelve** motivations or pitfalls. Each one is presented as a single concept with two faces — what makes it a *motivation* for the NPC who values it, and what makes it a *pitfall* for the NPC who rejects it. Each entry includes example arguments that appeal to the motivation.

> See [negotiation-stats.md](negotiation-stats.md) for how motivations and pitfalls interact with Interest and Patience, and [negotiation-arguments.md](negotiation-arguments.md) for argument resolution.

---

## 1. Benevolence

**Motivation.** The NPC believes in sharing what they have with others. An NPC in a negotiation must be limited in their benevolence so they don't simply hand the heroes whatever they need. Their charity might extend only to a specific group (a benevolent pirate captain shares plunder with her crew — but they're still plundering), or be limited because they don't have much to give.

**Pitfall.** A cynical view of the world: no creature has a right to anything just by being alive. Helping others because it's "the right thing" is a preposterous, immature, or inexperienced idea — to be laughed off or snuffed out.

**Example arguments:**
- *"If you lend us the Sword of Agathor, we can make Capital safer for your guild by using it to lay your enemies low."*
- *"If you can teleport us into the dragon's cave, we'll give you half the wyrm's hoard once we cut off the creature's head. That could benefit generations of students at your academy!"*

---

## 2. Discovery

**Motivation.** The NPC wants to learn new lore, explore forgotten places, break ground with new experiments, or uncover artifacts lost to time. Their curiosity might be focused (curing a rare disease, finding a portal to a far-off world) or unbounded.

**Pitfall.** No interest in finding new places, peoples, or ideas. The unknown might frighten or unsettle them, or a previous pursuit of discovery might have ended badly.

**Example arguments:**
- *"Allow us to use your cipher to translate the only copy of the Codex Mortis, and then we'll let you read the book when we're done."*
- *"We know the journey to Decant Isle is dangerous, but we're going into uncharted territory. We thought your crew of sailors might want to be among the few mortals to lay eyes on the place."*

---

## 3. Freedom

**Motivation.** The NPC wants no authority above them and desires no authority over others. They might already have personal freedom and want to maintain it, or they may want to liberate themself or others.

**Pitfall.** Believes a world without authority is one of turmoil and chaos. May even believe **they** should rule — that their ideals should become law.

**Example arguments:**
- *"I know you want to have the queen's authority revoked forever. She has no heirs. Give us the key to her study so that we can prove her corruption and give you a chance to topple the monarchy henceforth."*
- *"If you promise to give us ten vials of Assassin's Kiss, we'll see to it that the baron's prison is emptied."*

---

## 4. Greed

**Motivation.** Desires wealth and resources above almost anything else. Could be a miser who hoards but never spends, or an exhibitionist who flaunts wealth as a sign of station. Some have a greed for things other than money — a demon collecting souls, a troll lord hungering for flesh. Greed-driven NPCs sometimes share with a select few they love (a noble lord indulging his children).

**Pitfall.** No interest in accumulating wealth, and **offended** if anyone tries to buy their partnership. Holds ideals above material desires.

**Example arguments:**
- *"You should help us battle the overmind. Xorranox's wealth is legendary, and we'll see to it that you get your fair share."*
- *"Give us a week to do research among your private collection of books, and we'll give you another ten unique tomes we found in an ancient star elf sanctuary."*

---

## 5. Higher Authority

**Motivation.** Staunchly loyal to a person or force perceived as more important than themself — an organization, a deity or being of great power, a monarch or noble, a mystical presence, or an informal personal hero (older sibling, mentor).

**Pitfall.** Scoffs at the idea of serving anyone. May not believe everyone should be free — but they personally answer to no one.

**Example arguments:**
- *"All great creations honor your god, Malus. If you teach me to forge the Hammer of Azdul, that will be a great honor to bestow upon your god."*
- *"You know what Jarith the Bold would do? He'd guide us through the vast wasteland of the desert to reach the tower. Will you be our Jarith?"*

---

## 6. Justice

**Motivation.** Wants the righteous rewarded and the wicked punished — however subjective their sense of who is good or evil. A priest of a nature god might believe everyone who protects plants and animals is righteous, and that miners and lumberjacks must die. A justice motivation does **not** automatically make the NPC kind or charitable.

**Pitfall.** Doesn't believe the timescape is inherently just and has no interest in making it so. The world is eternal conflict; there is no such thing as justice; anyone who thinks otherwise is a naive fool.

**Example arguments:**
- *"You despise those who steal from nature. Allow us peacefully into your wode so we may bottle the Blessed Spring's water. We're going to use it to stop an army from felling every tree and tearing up the earth wherever they go."*
- *"You think nobles are lazy barons who get rich off the backs of peasants. We want to dethrone Lord Saxton. Lend us your crew of thieves, and we'll make sure that when Saxton falls, the people can choose their own leaders."*

---

## 7. Legacy

**Motivation.** Desires fame while alive and acclaim that lasts after death. Wants their deeds — great or terrible — known and remembered. Some seek immortality through deification or undeath so that mortality doesn't end their history-making.

**Pitfall.** Cares nothing about leaving a personal mark on the world. Such vain thinking is a waste of time.

**Example arguments:**
- *"If you give us the vizier's itinerary, I'll compose a song about your bravery in defying him, then sing it in every tavern from here to Ix!"*
- *"Yes, losing the battle is a possibility. If we do, the gnolls will still come for you eventually. But if we crush our foes, imagine the honors, the histories, the poems, the statues — all of it created for you because your siege engines turned the tide."*

---

## 8. Peace

**Motivation.** Wants calm in their life — to be left alone to run their business, farm, kingdom, criminal empire, or whatever small slice of the timescape is theirs. Some don't have peace and need help getting it; others want to keep what they have.

**Pitfall.** Hates being bored. Wants excitement, drama, and danger. Nothing is worse than the status quo.

**Example arguments:**
- *"You have a good thing going here. A little burgling of nobles, some alcohol smuggling, some illegal gambling dens. No one's getting hurt, but Constable Cofax is closing in on you. We could redirect him toward some real danger to the community, if you can help us set a trap for the Watchmaker."*
- *"I know you don't sell to outsiders, but we need that helm. I'm going to use it to turn back a group of hobgoblins marching this way. They're not going to be as friendly as us."*

---

## 9. Power

**Motivation.** Covets the authority of others. Wants to increase their influence, no matter how great it already is, and to maintain their domain. Might seek power through conquest, artifact collection, or supernatural ritual — though why pick one when all three together work best? Petty administrators and shrine-keepers can covet power as hungrily as world-traversing tyrants.

**Pitfall.** No interest in authority for themself. May respect others' authority but hates the thought of ruling and rejects any suggestion of it.

**Example arguments:**
- *"Everyone knows you should be running the watch, Percy. The old lady's retiring, and our friend Baron Kuglar is naming the replacement. Now, you let us into the restricted armory, and we'll put in a good word."*
- *"We know he's your brother, Your Highness, but he's older — first in line for the throne. If you help us prove he's in a cult, you become the favorite son."*

---

## 10. Protection

**Motivation.** Has land, people, information, items, or an organization they want protected above all else. Keeping the charge safe is a duty they hold dear — and aiding in that protection earns favor. Most people protect family, but a protection-motivated NPC believes in doing so **at any cost.**

**Pitfall.** Happy to leave others to fend for themselves. Doesn't believe it's their responsibility to protect anyone other than themself — and might be outright disgusted by the idea of risking their life or property for others.

**Example arguments:**
- *"Dead soldiers grow the necromancer's ranks. Total annihilation is the only way to defeat her. March with us now, while her army is small, and we'll defeat her. Or you could gamble that someone else tries, fails, and suddenly she's at the border, ready to overrun your kingdom with an army tenfold larger than what it is now."*
- *"I understand your grandchild is hell-bent on joining the service. I happen to have a magic suit of armor that could help them ward off the blows of monsters and ruffians. I'd be happy to give it to you, in exchange for borrowing your griffons for a few days. After all, I won't need the armor if I can simply fly over the marsh's monsters."*

---

## 11. Revelry

**Motivation.** Just wants to have fun. Enjoys socializing at parties, thrill-seeking, or other hedonistic pursuits. Pleasure with people they like is paramount.

**Pitfall.** Sees social encounters and hedonism as a waste of time. Takes pleasure only in work or self-improvement. Anyone suggesting immature debauchery is not worth their time.

**Example arguments:**
- *"How would you like to have the most exclusive songs for your exclusive birthday celebration next week? I'll write you a whole original set list, free of charge ... provided you extend me and my band here an invitation."*
- *"I know you don't want to forge five Chronokinesis Crowns. How's this instead? You do that for me, and I'll give you the fourteen kegs of whiskey we found in a steel-dwarf ruin. This stuff is old, unique, and forget-your-first-name potent. You can crack a keg with your friends to celebrate a job well done."*

---

## 12. Vengeance

**Motivation.** Wants to harm someone who has hurt them. Revenge can be proportional or escalated. Sometimes only death satisfies; other times, the NPC seeks embarrassment, career failure, or some less-permanent pain.

**Pitfall.** Believes revenge solves nothing. Either learned this firsthand, or simply lacks the ambition for vendetta — and looks down on those who pursue it.

**Example arguments:**
- *"The servants of Ajax killed your sister as she scoured the city for his cults. The Black Iron Pact works for the Overlord. Give us her diaries, and we might uncover the pact's hideaway and deal a great blow to your hated foes."*
- *"That prankster Huckable made your trousers tear at the last council meeting. Don't you want to pay him back? We can arrange a delicious prank at the next gathering, but we need you to guarantee the safety of the orc refugees."*
