# Draw Steel — Making Arguments

*Source: Draw Steel Heroes, Chapter 11: Negotiation, book pp. 287–288.*

As part of their initial request to an NPC in a negotiation, a hero makes an **argument** as to why the NPC should give them what they want. The argument can take several forms:

- An exchange — *"clear bandits from a forest, hand over a piece of treasure, slay a dragon for the NPC."*
- An appeal to self-interest — convincing the NPC it is in their own best interest to help.
- A moral imperative — appealing to a knight's sense of duty, the wealth a mercenary might earn, or the final wish of a queen's dearly departed grandmother.

NPCs who **admire** the heroes are more likely to respond to compliments and buttering up. Those who **fear** the heroes are more likely to respond to intimidation and awe.

## Arguments Need a Justification

Arguments need a **why** — a justification as to why they're true.

> *"Helping us defeat Lord Saxton is good for you in the long run."* — half an argument.
>
> *"Helping us defeat Lord Saxton is good for you in the long run, because we know he's coming for your kingdom after Bedegar falls!"* — full argument.

If a hero offers half an argument, the NPC may follow up with questions like *"Why do you say that?"* or *"What makes you think that's true?"* to draw out the rest.

## Who Makes the Argument

One hero makes an argument to the NPC, but the players can discuss the details out of character beforehand. It is up to the group how much around-the-table strategizing happens — some groups have the most fun without it; others want to plan often. Worth discussing with the group **before** the negotiation begins so everyone's on the same page.

> **Everyone Can Participate.** Because Reason and Intuition with creatively applied skills can be used to make arguments, **all heroes can actively participate** in negotiation. The hero with the highest Presence and the Persuade skill doesn't automatically have to be the one making every test.

## Appeal to Motivation

If an argument **doesn't include a pitfall** and **appeals to one of the NPC's motivations** that hasn't already been appealed to, the hero making the argument makes a **medium test**. Depending on the argument, this is a **Reason, Intuition, or Presence** test using any applicable skill — most commonly a skill from the **interpersonal** skill group.

> **Power Roll + Reason, Intuition, or Presence (medium):**
>
> - **Tier 1 (low):** The NPC's patience decreases by 1.
> - **Tier 2 (mid):** The NPC's interest increases by 1, **and** their patience decreases by 1.
> - **Tier 3 (high):** The NPC's interest increases by 1, and their patience **doesn't change**.

At the Director's discretion, a particularly **well-roleplayed or well-reasoned argument automatically counts as a tier 3 outcome without a test**. Good roleplaying should be rewarded.

If the heroes try to appeal to a motivation that **has already been successfully appealed to**, the NPC's interest stays the same and their patience decreases by 1.

> **Appealing to Multiple Motivations.** If an argument appears to appeal to more than one of the NPC's motivations, the Director can ask for clarification. After listing the motivations the argument seemed to invoke, they ask the player to pick **one**. If the player had a different motivation in mind, it is up to the Director whether the argument actually appealed to that specific motivation or not.

## No Motivation or Pitfall

If an argument **doesn't include any of the NPC's motivations or pitfalls**, the hero makes a **harder test** to sway the NPC.

> **Power Roll + Reason, Intuition, or Presence (hard):**
>
> - **Tier 1 (low):** The NPC's patience decreases by 1, **and** their interest decreases by 1.
> - **Tier 2 (mid):** The NPC's patience decreases by 1.
> - **Tier 3 (high):** The NPC's interest increases by 1, **and** their patience decreases by 1.
> - **Effect:** On a natural **19 or 20**, the NPC's patience remains the same.

If the heroes try the **same argument** without a pitfall or motivation **twice**, the test automatically obtains a **tier 1** outcome.

## Pitfall Used

If an argument uses one of the NPC's **pitfalls**, it **automatically fails**:

- The NPC's interest decreases by 1.
- The NPC's patience decreases by 1.
- The NPC might also warn the heroes not to treat them in such a way again.

(No roll is made — the bad outcome is automatic.)

## Caught in a Lie

If a hero **lies** to an NPC with an argument that **fails to increase** the NPC's interest, the Director can decide that the NPC catches the lie and is offended.

- The NPC's interest decreases by **1 additional** point on top of any decrease imposed by the failure.

## Renown and Negotiation

Renown determines whether a hero's fame (or infamy) carries weight with an NPC. A reputation can make negotiation easier — provided the hero knows how to use it.

During negotiation, the NPC has an **Impression score** that determines how much Renown is needed to influence them with fame alone. (See **Renown** in Chapter 13: Rewards.) Impression matters only if the NPC actually **knows of** the heroes:

- A dragon woken from a hundred-year slumber and immediately dragged into negotiation **cannot** be influenced by Renown — they don't know who the heroes are. (And waking a dragon to negotiate is a terrible idea regardless.)
- If the NPC knows of the hero **and** has an Impression score equal to or **less than** the hero's Renown, the NPC can be influenced by that hero's reputation.

Higher Impression = harder to influence with Renown alone. A small-time brigand has a lower Impression than a monarch who meets famous and powerful people daily.

If a creature has a level, their **Impression score equals their level** unless the Director decides otherwise.

### Fame or Infamy?

If a hero has enough Renown to influence an NPC, the **Director decides** whether the hero is famous or infamous to that NPC:

- **Famous** — the NPC appreciates the hero's deeds and views them as making the world a better place.
- **Infamous** — the NPC believes the hero's accomplishments make the world worse and views them as an enemy.

### Influencing Tests

- If the hero is **famous** to the NPC, they gain an **edge** on tests for arguments to which **Flirt, Lead, or Persuade** could apply.
- If the hero is **infamous** to the NPC, they gain an **edge** on tests for arguments to which **Brag, Interrogate, or Intimidate** could apply.

A hero gains this edge **even if they don't actually have the appropriate skill** — what matters is whether the skill *could* apply to the argument.
