# Draw Steel — Montages

Ruleset reference for `exploration-encounter`. Source: *Draw Steel Heroes* v1, Chapter 9, book pp. 261–263.

## When to use

Heroes work together **over time** toward a common goal that **requires more than one characteristic**. Typical uses: cross a desert, rally farmers against a tyrant, perform a ritual to open a sealed gate, prepare village defenses, long chase across a landscape.

**Only run a montage when stakes and pressure exist** (deadline, impending harm, rival racing you). Low- or no-stakes travel can be narrated in montage *style* without needing a montage test.

**Same-task vs. multi-task** — if heroes all do the *same* task in one moment, use a [[grouptest|Group Test]] instead.

## Structure

### Director sets the scene

Describe the scenario and the challenges the heroes might face — the "obstacle pool" players will draw from (dunes, sandstorms, quicksand, chasms for a desert; throngs, carts, stray dogs for a market chase). This frames what heroes can attempt.

### Round loop

Montages run in **rounds**. Per round, each hero takes **one turn** (any order), choosing one of:

- **Make a test** — propose characteristic + skill + approach; Director sets difficulty.
- **Assist another hero's test** (see [[skills|Assist a Test]]).
- **Use an item, ability, or other option.** Sufficiently clever uses can grant **automatic successes** with no roll (example: a magic fan keeping a ship's sails full day and night = 2 auto-successes).
- **Do nothing.** Usually when there's no one to assist and acting risks making things worse.

Once a hero acts, they can't act again that round until **every other participant has had a chance.** Round ends when everyone has acted; start the next round.

### 2 rounds by default

Montages last **2 rounds** unless the success/failure limit is hit first. The cap prevents slogs and discourages "pile every turn on the one best hero." Director can extend for a grueling challenge.

## Individual tests in a montage

Each test follows the normal [[tests|test]] rules. Per-test difficulty can vary (one hero's obstacle is easy, another's hard, in the same round).

**Can't reuse a skill per hero.** A single character can't use the same skill more than once in a montage — that skill's +2 represents their entire contribution on that skill. Multiple *different* heroes can each use the same skill. Director can lift this for very long or narrowly-scoped montages.

**New challenges per test.** Each test should target an obstacle **not already overcome**. Continuous challenges (fighting fire through a rescue) can be exceptions.

**Director can introduce new challenges mid-montage** as the fiction escalates (beams fall, windows explode, temperature spikes).

**Individual rewards/consequences apply normally** — use hero-token and +2-Malice defaults to keep pacing, or craft task-specific ones (e.g., a failing hero takes a bane on their next test).

## Success/failure thresholds

Every montage has a **success limit** and **failure limit** — the finish lines. Director can share them openly or keep them secret.

### Difficulty table (5-hero group)

| Difficulty | Success Limit | Failure Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 5 | 5 |
| Moderate | 6 | 4 |
| Hard | 7 | 3 |

### Group-size adjustments

- **4 or fewer heroes** — decrease both limits by **1 per hero below 5** (minimum 2 each).
- **6 or more heroes** — increase both limits by **1 per hero above 5**.

## Outcomes

| Outcome | Condition | Narrative | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total success** | Hit success limit before failure limit and before time runs out. | Goal achieved without complication. | 1 Victory (easy/moderate), 2 Victories (hard). |
| **Partial success** | Hit failure limit *or* time runs out, AND have ≥2 more successes than failures. | Goal achieved, but with a complication or cost. | 1 Victory (moderate/hard); none on easy. |
| **Total failure** | Hit failure limit *or* time runs out, AND don't have ≥2 more successes than failures. | Goal not achieved — but per [[tests|fail-forward]], this should make the story more interesting, not end it. | None. |

## Interlude pattern

A montage can be paused mid-round for **combat, negotiation, or other scenes**, then resumed. Example: kingfissure worm attacks between rounds 1 and 2 of a desert-crossing montage; after the fight, the montage continues from where it left off.
