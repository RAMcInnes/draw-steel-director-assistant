# Draw Steel — Tests

Ruleset reference for `exploration-encounter`. Source: *Draw Steel Heroes* v1, Chapter 9, book pp. 249–253, 257.

## What is a Test

A test is any power roll with failure or consequences on the table. Call for one only when failure is **interesting or dramatic and won't stall the story**. Clever, outside-the-box approaches can skip the test entirely ("It Just Works!"); the Director can require a test on reuse of the same trick.

## Resolving a Test

1. Director picks **characteristic** and **difficulty** (easy, medium, hard). Easier than easy → no test. Harder than hard → impossible.
2. Player rolls **2d10 + characteristic**. If a skill applies and the Director agrees, add **+2** (see [[skills]]).
3. Apply edges/banes.
4. Compare total to the Test Difficulty Outcomes table.

- **Rerolls** — spend a hero token; must use the new roll.
- **Skill +2 is not an edge** — a test can stack both.
- **Only one skill per test.**

### Characteristics

| Characteristic | Typical uses |
|---|---|
| Might | Force doors, hurl heavy objects, raw-strength climbs, swim against current. |
| Agility | Tumble, sneak, pick locks, sleight of hand. |
| Reason | Recall lore, deduce from clues, puzzles, forgery, codes, logical argument. |
| Intuition | Notice hidden creatures/details, read motives, calm or train animals. |
| Presence | Gain trust, project confidence, influence, lead. |

Skills flex across characteristics — intimidating verbally is Presence, tearing a log in half to do it is Might; Intimidate applies either way.

## Test Difficulty Outcomes

| Power Roll | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 11 | Success with a consequence | Failure | Failure with a consequence |
| 12–16 | Success | Success with a consequence | Failure |
| 17+ | Success with a reward | Success | Success with a consequence |
| Natural 19 or 20 | Success with a reward | Success with a reward | Success with a reward |

A **natural 19 or 20** on the raw 2d10 (before mods) always yields success with a reward, regardless of difficulty.

"A success" covers all three success rows. "A failure" covers both failure rows.

## Outcome shapes

- **Failure with a consequence** — task fails + impactful setback. Defaults: an NPC storms off / betrays / attacks, foes notice, a trap triggers, gear breaks, hero believes a falsehood, a new negotiation or montage is now required. Abstract default: Director gains **+2 Malice** at the start of the next combat. Consequences can be latent (a cheat noticed at cards, acted on hours later).
- **Failure** — task fails, no cost. Director may add a minor sting (one cultist drifts over, hasn't raised the alarm) and may offer **"succeed with a consequence instead"**; player chooses.
- **Success with a consequence** — task succeeds + added cost (wall unstable for next climber; sneak left footprints; bribed guard demands a gemstone). Abstract default: +2 Malice next combat. Director may offer **"fail instead and skip the cost"**; player chooses.
- **Success** — task succeeds cleanly.
- **Success with a reward** — task succeeds + boon. Defaults: follow-up task auto-succeeds; an ally in the same task succeeds without rolling; consumable or useful gear; helpful info; small favor from an impressed onlooker; notice a hidden danger in time. Abstract default: players gain a **hero token**.

**Optional — Pitching.** Players can pitch their own consequences/rewards; Director vetoes or edits.

### Opposed-difficulty heuristic

- **Easy** — one opposer with lower bonus than hero.
- **Moderate** — multiple lower-bonus opposers, or one equal-bonus opposer.
- **Hard** — any higher-bonus opposer, or multiple equal-bonus opposers.

## Opposed Power Rolls

When both sides test a dramatic struggle, everyone rolls; **highest total wins**. No rewards. No tier structure.

**Tie** — scene state doesn't change.

**Tier-less modifiers** (no outcome tiers exist here):
- Double edge / automatic tier-up → **+4**
- Double bane / automatic tier-down → **−4**

## Reactive Tests

Director asks for a test *without context*, reveals purpose only after the roll. Use when the player doesn't know to ask.

- **Hidden features** — Intuition for secret doors, disguised traps.
- **Hidden foes** — Intuition to spot them (or NPCs-Roll-for-Deceptive-Tasks, below).
- **Hidden motives** — Intuition to read an NPC.
- **Recall Lore** — Reason on new objects, info, events.
- **Creature / DTO tests** — forced by monsters or dynamic terrain objects in *Draw Steel: Monsters*; **can't be modified by skills**.

**Optional — Secret Reactive Tests.** Director rolls these for the players.

## Who rolls

**Heroes roll; NPCs usually don't.** Exceptions:
- **NPC retainers** test occasionally.
- **Deceptive tasks** — Director rolls secretly for NPC deception (assassin sneaking up on unwatched camp = Agility; lying NPC = Presence).
- **Opposed power rolls** (above).
- **Reactive tests** (above).

## Ancillary rules

- **Duration is Director's call** — recall lore = instant; duck behind a barrel = a maneuver; track across the World Below = days.
- **Tests in combat** often resolve as maneuvers; see Chapter 10.
- **No retry on failure** unless circumstances meaningfully change (better tools, instruction, different approach). Director decides what counts as "changed."

## PC agency

**PCs aren't influenced by any creature's test** — no NPC's Presence test compels a hero, and one PC generally can't Intuition another. A table can lift this by consensus.

## Fail-forward principle

Failed tests **reshape the scene, not end it.** When designing a test, name what happens on each outcome tier — if any ends the fiction dead, redesign. Prefer consequences that change the route (now alerted, now a different skill unlocks the door, now someone's in debt) over consequences that close it.
