# Draw Steel Encounter Objectives

Source: *Draw Steel: Monster Basics*, pp. 15–24. Objectives let combat be about more than hacking foes to 0 Stamina. Start with the objective, then pick enemies, map, and monster roles that make it a fun challenge.

Each objective writeup uses these sections:
- **Monster Roles and Organization** — which creature types fit the objective.
- **Map Advice** — what to look for when choosing the battle map.
- **Difficulty Modifier** — how the objective shifts encounter difficulty up or down (usually one category).
- **Success Condition** — what the heroes must do to win. When met, remaining enemies flee, surrender, or a dramatic finish is used.
- **Victories** — Victories each hero earns based on difficulty.
- **Failure Condition** — what happens when the heroes fail.

## General guidance

**Making objectives known.** Players do best when they know what they're working toward, but you don't have to announce it. Often the objective is obvious (defeat the necromancer ends the undead). When it isn't (defeating a leader causes the band to fall apart), plant a hint — have the leader bark orders or shame the others.

**Fun is most important.** If heroes achieve the objective fast but are still enjoying the fight, don't call it off — have foes make a last stand, drop reinforcements, or reveal a new objective. Conversely, if a clever plan ends a fight early, let it.

**Choosing a map.** Prefer maps with ledges, columns, walls, windows, dynamic terrain, difficult terrain, and verticality. Avoid long 1-square corridors. Use flat maps only for dramatic showdowns.

## Diminish Numbers

Classic "defeat them before they defeat us." Heroes don't have to kill every foe — they just need to break, rout, or force surrender of the opposition.

- **Monster Roles and Organization:** mix all roles. Artillery in hard-to-reach spots, defenders paired with controllers/hexers, brutes on the front line.
- **Map Advice:** standard map guidance. Favor terrain that suits your chosen creatures; give space to move if the creature count is high.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** none.
- **Success Condition** (pick one):
  - With 2+ groups of minions: ends when no non-minion enemies remain.
  - Mostly horde: ends when heroes outnumber foes.
  - Mostly platoon: ends when heroes outnumber foes 2:1.
  - Ends when remaining foes are half or less of the start count.
  - Solo creature: flees/surrenders at ≤¼ Stamina after using all villain actions.
- **Victories:** 1 for easy/standard, 2 for hard/extreme.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if heroes are killed, captured, flee, or fail to defeat their foes.

**Fleeing Foes (sidebar).** When success triggers, most enemies flee. Don't habitually bring fleeing foes back with reinforcements — it trains players to grind every fight to the bitter end. If a specific foe will return, signal it clearly (e.g., swear vengeance as they flee) so players know *that* one matters and the rest can be ignored.

## Defeat a Specific Foe

One or more enemies are the stars (a hobgoblin bloodlord leading mercenaries, a pair of tusker demons in a gnoll band). Once the stars fall, weaker foes flee or surrender.

- **Monster Roles and Organization:** the specific foe is typically a leader, a high-Stamina brute, or any role as an elite or higher-level creature. Give them a defender or two and some minions for protection.
- **Map Advice:** protect the foe's placement — don't leave them exposed on all sides. Ranged foes want high ground and choke points. Strike-and-retreat foes want cover and space. If you fear teleport attacks, use walls/barriers (teleport needs line of effect). Give the foe a plausible escape route, often a choke point; if heroes pursue, have allies hold them off but don't make pursuit impossible.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** if the designated foes are ≤⅓ of total EV, difficulty drops one category.
- **Success Condition:** designated foe(s) reduced to 0 Stamina.
- **Victories:** 1 for easy/standard, 2 for hard.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if any designated creature is not defeated — including if they choose to flee once allies start dropping.

## Get the Thing!

Heroes race to collect a valuable item from a guarded location, or steal it from enemies already holding it.

- **The Thing:** typically a size 1T object. (Bulky objects that must be carried past enemies lean toward Escort.) Can be fragile (paper) or near-unbreakable (magic weapon); enemies don't want it harmed but accidents happen.
- **Extra defenses (optional):**
  - **Hidden:** heroes need successful tests to find it, or it's behind a door/sealed vessel, or hidden in plain sight among identical decoys.
  - **Held:** carried by an enemy. If the thing is a weapon or implement, expect it to be used against the heroes.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** guards are typically leaders or brutes/defenders. Hidden ambushers are devious. Harriers and mounts chase heroes fleeing with the thing; controllers change terrain; hexers slow heroes; minions clog hallways.
- **Map Advice:** place the thing where it's defended by hazards and choke points — never near an escape route. Use a big map; escape is part of the challenge.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** one easier if no powerful monster (≥⅓ of total EV) or trap directly guards the thing. One harder if the thing is hidden or held.
- **Success Condition:** all heroes leave the map with the thing.
- **Victories:** 1 for easy/standard/hard. 2 for extreme, or if no hero took damage during a standard-or-higher encounter.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if the thing is destroyed or stays in enemy possession.

## Destroy the Thing!

Burn the pirate's ship, close a planar portal, shut down a trap array. Destroying the thing may end combat entirely.

- **The Thing:** object with Stamina = heroes' level × 35 (×50 if **Sturdy**). If **Multiple**, divide that Stamina across the things. Most objects have poison and psychic immunity, and may have immunity or weakness to specific damage types.
- **Extra defenses (optional):**
  - **Hidden:** tests needed to find it, or hidden behind a door / sealed vessel / in plain sight.
  - **Held:** carried by an enemy (can be used against heroes). Can't be both hidden and held.
  - **Sturdy:** Stamina = level × 50.
  - **Multiple:** several things, all must be destroyed.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** beefy defenders — brutes, defenders, minion squads. Artillery harassing from afar forces melee heroes to choose between the thing and the ranged threat. Add ambushers if heroes rely on ranged attacks.
- **Map Advice:** thing should be easy to protect, hard to approach. Hidden entrances, teleport platforms, or portals let monsters close in once heroes reach the target. Smaller maps work well.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** one easier if no extra defenses. One harder if 2+ extra defenses.
- **Success Condition:** destroy the thing (or a required count in a multi-thing setup).
- **Victories:** 1 for easy/standard, 2 for hard.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if the thing isn't destroyed.

## Save Another

Rescue NPCs from foes — sometimes freed allies then join the fight and make the rest of the encounter trivial.

- **Allies and Potential Allies:** *willing allies* fight alongside heroes (heroes control them; may use retainer stat blocks). *Potential allies* won't join until won over and freed — requires a successful hard Presence test as a maneuver. Until then, potential allies try to flee on their turns and are GM-controlled.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** controllers and hexers raise the difficulty of keeping others alive. Harriers and mounts chase fleeing NPCs; minions cut off escape routes; artillery targets NPCs from afar. If NPCs start captured, brutes make good hard-to-kill guards.
- **Map Advice:** NPCs placed somewhere hard to reach, away from escape routes, protected by choke points monsters can use. Provide 2+ escape routes on different sides so fleeing NPCs scatter. Big map — NPCs have to actually flee the area.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** for each willing ally at heroes' level or higher, add one hero to the party for difficulty calculation.
- **Success Condition:** at least half of all willing + potential allies survived *and* all were saved — joined the fight or retreated off the map.
- **Victories:** 1 if success condition met on easy/standard. 2 if hard/extreme, or if *all* NPCs survived on standard or higher.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if more than half the NPCs were killed or captured.

## Escort

Protect a **ward** (NPC, animal, or bulky object) as it travels to a destination.

- **Ward:** at the start of each round, players pick which hero is responsible. The ward moves on that hero's turn and is controlled by that player. Wards can take a move action *or* a maneuver on their turn, not both. Most humanoid wards have speed 5.
  - Sturdy ward (active humanoid): Stamina = heroes' level × 20.
  - Delicate ward (elderly diplomat, important object): Stamina = heroes' level × 10.
- **Destination:** a place of safety past which enemies won't follow. The ward's starting distance from the destination should be at least 3× the ward's speed (closer if terrain complicates the route). Enemies can start anywhere, including between ward and destination, but not within 5 squares of the destination.
- **Reinforcements:** at the start of each combat round, enemies killed last round are replaced by a new group whose total EV is within 5 of what was lost. GM places them; none start within 5 squares of the ward, destination, or any hero.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** harriers and mounts catch up; minions act as mobile obstacles; support creatures boost their speed; controllers place terrain; hexers slow/pin the ward; ambushers lying in wait on the route are always solid.
- **Map Advice:** big, with obstructions, cover, and hazards. Heroes shouldn't just walk a road — climb cliffs, cross water/lava, push through brambles, navigate twisting corridors. Match hazards to ward weaknesses if any. Indoor routes benefit from dead ends.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** one harder for a delicate ward.
- **Success Condition:** ward reaches the destination.
- **Victories:** 1 on success. 2 if extreme, or if ward + all heroes reach the destination within 3 rounds on standard or higher.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if the ward hits 0 Stamina or is prevented from reaching the destination.

## Hold Them Off

Buy time. Protect a position for a fixed number of rounds while something else happens (villagers escape, priests complete a ritual).

- **Defensive Position:** GM chooses (often with player input) — any size. Enemies try to get through or reach a point inside/around it. Fewer enemies through = better odds. Position may grant benefits (high ground, choke point, difficult terrain approach, climb required). Optionally use 2+ positions for a harder encounter.
- **Encounter Duration:** GM-set number of rounds; typical is 3.
- **Reinforcements:** at the end of each combat round, add enemies 10+ squares from the defensive position. New group's EV = EV of enemies who got past last round + EV of one hero. Unused EV can roll into a future round.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** all roles work well. Controllers reshape terrain and move heroes out of position. Ambushers, harriers, mounts slip past (especially organized as minions or hordes). Artillery harasses from afar to pull heroes out. Leaders and supports grant extra movement and attacks. Use fliers/burrowers/teleporters only occasionally to stay fair.
- **Map Advice:** defensive position has ≥2 openings, each 3+ squares wide. Not so wide it can't be defended, not so narrow it's trivially locked down. Approach should have cover so monsters aren't just charging uphill. Large enough that monsters can't rush and overwhelm, small enough that they can reach the position within 3 rounds.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** difficulty set by creatures present at the start of round 1 (don't count reinforcements). One harder if duration ≥5 rounds or 3+ defensive positions.
- **Success Condition:** heroes survive the full duration *and* fewer creatures got through/into the position than the starting hero count (including retainers).
- **Victories:** 1 on success. 2 if extreme, or if held for 5+ rounds on standard or higher.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if a number of creatures equal to or greater than the starting hero count got through or into the desired area.

## Assault the Defenses

The inverse of Hold Them Off — heroes take a defensive position from the enemy. Often part of a combined objective (assault, then hold against counterattack).

- **Defensive Position:** GM-chosen area heroes must capture — any size. Not all enemies are inside; some patrol outside to stop the approach. Position typically grants defender benefits: choke points, difficult terrain, climbing/swimming approaches, high ground.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** outside patrol of defenders and brutes protects the site; inside, some mix of artillery, controllers, and hexers hits heroes on approach. Support creature or leader buffs everyone else.
- **Map Advice:** same qualities as Hold Them Off, with roles reversed.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** one harder if the defensive position grants 2+ distinct defender benefits.
- **Success Condition:** one or more heroes and no enemies have been inside the defensive position for 4 consecutive turns.
- **Victories:** 1 on success, 2 if hard/extreme.
- **Failure Condition:** heroes fail only if they can't achieve success.

## Stop the Action

Interrupt villainous work in progress — a ritual, a wedding, an execution. Has a timer; if the heroes don't stop it in time, the conditions of the battle change.

- **Encounter Duration:** GM-set rounds before the villains complete their plan. Typical is 3. Events may change the duration (a hard Reason test disputing a wedding's legality might add a round; failing to protect a ritual sacrifice might subtract one).
- **Stopping the Action:** the plan has requirements — if unmet, it stops. E.g., a ritual requires one or more zealot priests to spend a main action each round continuing it; a wedding can't complete if the participant or officiant is killed or captured.
- **Failure Consequences:** if the plan succeeds, extra consequences can unfold mid-encounter (summoned demon, newly crowned mage calls guards). Reinforcements of this kind aren't counted in the original difficulty — treat it as a new battle.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** depends on the action. If stopping involves destroying/capturing/reaching a creature or object: brutes and defenders keep heroes away; artillery and harriers target them anywhere on the map.
- **Map Advice:** depends on the action, but reaching the critical spot shouldn't be trivial. Hazardous terrain, walls, and cover prevent walk-up neutralization.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** one harder if duration ≤2 rounds. One easier if the action can be stopped by killing or neutralizing any *one* creature.
- **Success Condition:** stop the action before the duration expires.
- **Victories:** 1 on success. 2 if extreme, or if stopped before the last round on standard or higher.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories if the action completes. Heroes might earn Victories from whatever new encounter results from the failure.

**Under Pressure (sidebar).** Stop the Action pairs well with other objectives when heroes are breezing through — "diminish numbers within 2 rounds or lose the infiltration window," "escort within 3 rounds before the explosion." See Combined Objectives.

## Complete the Action

The mirror of Stop the Action — heroes must *perform* an action (launch an airship, cast a ritual) while fighting off interference.

- **Encounter Duration:** GM-set; typical is 3 rounds.
- **Tasks and Failures:** each round, half or more of the heroes must use a maneuver to perform a task that advances the plan. GM decides what tasks are available and where. Available tasks can change turn to turn. For harder encounters, tasks require a successful test. At the end of any round where the required number of tasks wasn't done, the party accumulates one failure.
- **Monster Roles and Organization:** controllers and hexers prevent task completion, especially backed by supports. Brutes block key spots; ambushers harass and dance away.
- **Map Advice:** task locations shouldn't be adjacent; moving between them should be complicated.
- **Difficulty Modifier:** one harder if duration ≥5 rounds or tasks require tests. One easier if duration ≤2 rounds.
- **Success Condition:** reach the end of the duration with 1 or 0 failures.
- **Victories:** 1 on success. 2 if extreme, or if the party ended with zero failures on standard or higher.
- **Failure Condition:** no Victories and the action can't be completed if the party accumulates 2+ failures during the duration.

## Combined Objectives

An encounter can require multiple objectives — all of them met to win. Example: break the prison gates (Destroy the Thing) *and* hold off the guards while prisoners escape (Hold Them Off). Gates standing blocks escape; broken gates without escape is pointless — all or nothing. Each hero earns Victories as appropriate for each included objective.

---

## Alternative Objectives

One encounter, several valid success paths. Example: heroes face an evil emperor channeling a magic crown. They can win by killing or capturing the emperor (Defeat a Specific Foe), by breaking or stealing the crown (Destroy the Thing / Get the Thing), or by negotiating a detente. Reward the objective the heroes completed most successfully — if they stole the crown, it doesn't matter that the other conditions weren't met.

---

## Changing Objectives

Start with one objective, add or shift to another for dramatic tension. Example: heroes are fighting a demon lord (Defeat a Specific Foe), then notice cultists about to sacrifice innocents — objective shifts to Save Another. Make the shift clear: as long as the innocents live, the demon lord surviving no longer matters.

**Changing or Adding Objectives (sidebar).** If heroes are having an exceptionally easy time, a mid-battle twist that adds an objective is a reliable lever. Don't do this every fight — lean on it when an encounter you meant to be hard turns trivial.
