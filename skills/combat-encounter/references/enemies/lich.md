# Lich

*Source: Draw Steel Monsters, pp. 279–280. Reference only — stat blocks live in the book.*

A mortal once — usually a studied mage, consumed by an unquenchable hunger for knowledge beyond life, death, worlds, gods, and fiends — who performed a forbidden, evil ritual and anchored their soul into an external object to escape dying. A lich never truly dies as long as that anchor survives.

- **Forging a Soulstone.** The ritual requires a sphere built of something personal to the subject (steel from an heirloom suit of armor, branches from a wedding tree, etc.). The would-be lich then tears out their *own* heart and the heart of a living loved one, places both inside the sphere, and the two hearts transmute into a ruby that welds the sphere into a soulstone. As long as the soulstone remains intact, the lich cannot be permanently killed. Most soulstones are small and easily concealed — but the outer sphere can be built at any scale, from a pendant to a room-sized monument.
- **Terrifying Immortality.** The self-inflicted chest wound never heals. Over centuries, the lich's body decays into a skeleton with a hole where the heart used to be. Some liches hide behind illusions that make them more attractive and vivacious than they were in life; others embrace the corpse-look as a trophy of their achievement.
- **Eldritch Lairs.** Most liches are surrounded by devoted followers — legions of undead, mind-warped cultists, or amoral mercenary companies guarding their most important holdings. Lairs can be strongholds, castles, hidden bank vaults, government halls, or false temples. Savvier liches scatter multiple lairs across continents or even worlds so that adventurers destroying one sanctum don't threaten the whole.
- **Merciless Schemers.** Liches know adventurers are the single biggest threat to their soulstone and their plans, so they hunt powerful mortals the way hunters stalk tigers — with patience, research, and no mercy. A lich who has spent centuries refining hatred into an art form is a bad long-term enemy.
- **Languages.** A lich speaks whatever they knew in life, and picks up Anjali, Proto-Ctholl, Variac, and Vastariax across their extended existence.

### Director's note on running a lich encounter

Liches are designed around the **Rejuvenation** clause (see shared traits). A party that destroys a lich's body without first destroying the soulstone will discover that the lich reconstitutes. The classic lich encounter is therefore a *two-stage* adventure:
1. **Find and destroy the soulstone first.** This is typically a dungeon delve into a secondary lair — or a series of chases and puzzles leading to one. The lich is *not present* during this phase, because they've scattered the soulstone from the body on purpose. Soulstones are sonic- and holy-damage-destructible, immune to everything else.
2. **Then fight the lich.** Once the soulstone is destroyed, the Level 10 Solo combat encounter is winnable. Skipping step one turns the fight into an infinite loop.

If the party does discover an *inhabited* soulstone (after killing the body but before destroying the stone), the **Rejuvenation** compulsion creates an entirely different scene — see shared traits below.

## Shared traits (solo creature)

- **Solo Monster.** *End Effect* — at end of each turn, the lich takes 20 unresistable damage to end one save-ends effect. *Solo Turns* — two turns per round, never consecutive. Single-round lockdown is impossible.
- **Herald of Oblivion** (passive). In the lich's presence, death's call is stronger. *Any winded creature within 5 squares of the lich is bleeding and can't use the Catch Breath maneuver.* The endgame turns against the party — once heroes go winded near the lich, they bleed out and can't recover.
- **Glare of Undeath** (passive). At start of each round, the lich picks a creature within 10 squares. If that creature has R<4, they're restrained until end of the lich's next turn. Can't pick the same target two rounds in a row. Free anti-caster / anti-Reason-focused-hero cycle.
- **Rejuvenation** (the lich's actual survival mechanism). The lich's soulstone has 50 Stamina and damage immunity to everything *except sonic and holy*. If the lich is destroyed while the soulstone is intact, their soul retreats into the soulstone.
  - Any creature with P<5 who moves within 5 squares of an inhabited soulstone for the first time in a round, or starts their turn there, is **compelled save-ends** — they must do everything in their power to move toward and touch the soulstone.
  - A creature who touches an inhabited soulstone makes a Might test *with a bane*. Tier 1 result: the creature is reduced to 0 Stamina and the lich manifests adjacent with full Stamina. Tier 2: the creature is reduced to 0 Stamina and the lich manifests with 300 Stamina. Tier 3: the creature's Stamina drops to their winded value and the lich manifests with 100 Stamina.
  - So even a successful creature-touching-soulstone test resurrects the lich. Only *destroying* the soulstone (with sonic or holy damage) permanently ends it.

## Lich Malice features

Spent at the start of the lich's turn.

- **Soul Sip (3 Malice, Maneuver).** The lich makes a free strike against an enemy within 20 squares, then gains an edge on their next power roll. Cheap damage + setup for the next Signature.
- **Solo Action (5 Malice).** The lich takes an additional main action this turn *even if dazed.* Dazed-proof turn-economy button.
- **Spirit Shell (5 Malice, Maneuver).** A swirling cloud of angry spirits surrounds the lich until start of next round. Any creature who damages the lich with a melee strike while the shell is active makes a Presence test: tier 1 takes 16 psychic + dazed save-ends, tier 2 takes dazed save-ends, tier 3 no effect. The "stop hitting me" button.
- **Cloud of Deceit (7 Malice).** The lich summons a mind-altering mist, turns invisible until start of their next turn, and moves up to half speed. Each enemy within 20 squares of the lich's *starting point* with I<4 is *deceived* — they perceive all allies and enemies as the lich, and the environment as an unfamiliar necrotic wasteland. Communication from outside comes across as ghastly taunts in the lich's voice. Effect ends when an affected creature takes damage, or an ally uses a main action to shake them out. **Each time Cloud of Deceit is used in the encounter, the distance decreases by 5 (minimum 5) and the potency increases by 1.** So the lich's first deception hits a wide party but is relatively escape-able; the third or fourth deception is small-radius and nearly unavoidable.

## Lich — solo

**Role:** solo
**Tags:** level 10, solo, undead
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 280

**Flavor:** Size 1M, Stamina 650, Speed 10, Fly/hover, Stability 1, Free Strike 10. Corruption and poison immunity 10, *holy weakness 5*. Reason, Intuition, and Presence all +5 — the lich outthinks every hero at the table.

**Tactics:**

**Conflagration** (Signature, Main action, Ranged 20, two targets) — fire damage across tiers with *immolated* save-ends on failed Agility tests. *An immolated creature takes 10 fire damage whenever they use a main action and a maneuver on their turn, and this damage can't be reduced in any way.* Punishes heroes for acting normally — an immolated hero has to choose between efficient action economy and taking 10 chip damage.

**Hopeless Place** (Main action, 10-burst area) — corruption damage with *hopeless* save-ends on failed Presence tests across tiers. A hopeless creature can't benefit from edges or double edges, can't gain or use surges, and can't gain temporary Stamina. Shuts down hero resource systems. **3 Malice upgrade:** 20-burst area and +1 potency.

**Pain Unending** (2 Malice, Main action, Ranged 20) — psychic damage with *wracked with pain* save-ends on failed Might tests. A wracked-with-pain creature has a double bane on abilities. **3 Malice upgrade:** one additional target. **2+ Malice rider:** each wracked-with-pain creature gains one of bleeding, slowed, or prone-and-can't-stand per 2 additional Malice spent (lich's choice), each ending when the target is no longer wracked with pain.

**Necrotic Form** (Maneuver, Self) — the lich becomes spectral, moves up to their speed, and becomes corporeal again. While spectral, the lich *automatically ends grabbed and restrained*, has damage immunity 5, can move through solid matter, and ignores difficult terrain. If the lich ends inside solid matter, they're shunted back out into the space they entered from. Every escape answer to every melee-lock strategy.

**Baleful Swap** (2 Malice, triggered action, Ranged 10) — when the lich is targeted with an ability by a creature other than the target, if the target has P<4, they swap places with the lich and become the new target of the triggering ability. Heroes aiming at the lich find themselves hitting the ally next to them.

Villain actions:

- **Cages of Wasting (VA1, two 3-cubes within 10).** Each creature in the area makes an Agility test — tier 1 is 10 corruption + restrained save-ends, tier 2 is 16 corruption + restrained EoT, tier 3 is 20 corruption. *Each creature restrained this way takes an additional 10 corruption from the lich.* The "lock two pairs of heroes in place" opener.
- **My Power Alone (VA2, 12-burst area).** Each enemy target can't use heroic abilities until start of the lich's next turn. The party's big-play button gets cut off for a full turn — spend when heroes are setting up a heroic-resource kill combo. Combined with Solo Action Malice and Hopeless Place, the lich can functionally mute hero resources for one round and act twice.
- **Arms of Necrosis (VA3, 6-burst area).** Each creature in the area takes corruption damage with *frightened* save-ends on failed Agility tests. *At end of each of the lich's turns, they regain 10 Stamina for each creature frightened this way.* The endgame self-heal — frighten the whole party in a burst and heal 60+ Stamina per round while the fear lingers.

Counterplay: (1) **The fight is the second half of the encounter.** Destroy the soulstone first, with sonic or holy damage, before engaging the lich directly. (2) **Holy damage is the weakness.** Lich has holy weakness 5 and the soulstone is holy-destructible — a cleric or paladin is effectively mandatory. (3) **Herald of Oblivion + Catch Breath denial** means heroes who go winded near the lich are in real danger of bleeding out. Stay above winded, and if a hero drops below, get them out of the 5-square aura before end of their turn. (4) **Rejuvenation's compelled clause** can actively trap heroes — a low-Presence character who sees an inhabited soulstone may march themselves into it. Keep the highest-Presence heroes forward during soulstone-destruction phases. (5) **Glare of Undeath** restrains one low-Reason hero per round; if a caster is the Reason-low target, protect line of effect. (6) **Cloud of Deceit compounds** — the more times the lich casts it in one encounter, the harder it is to resist. Try to break the fight into short decisive windows so the lich doesn't get to cycle it 3+ times. (7) **The soulstone's location is the campaign hook.** Run the lich as a multi-session arc: investigation → lair infiltration → soulstone destruction → direct combat. A lich encounter as a single session almost always ends with the party discovering Rejuvenation the hard way.
