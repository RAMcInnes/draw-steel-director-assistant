# Medusa

*Source: Draw Steel Monsters, pp. 204–205. Reference only — stat blocks live in the book.*

Mortals once — humanoids who defied a god and were cursed to live out that defiance with reptilian skin, serpentine eyes, and a nest of venomous black snakes where their hair used to be. A medusa's gaze can slowly turn flesh to stone. Some embrace the curse and the cruelty that comes with it; many just want to be left alone and fight only when ignorance or myth drives hero-bands to their doorstep.

- **Cursed for Defiance.** The curse is divine and specific: the followers of the god who punished them spread terrifying myths about medusas, which pushes medusas into solitude or into the company of creatures who don't judge them by appearance. Many medusas spend decades researching lost rituals to break the curse — or retributive magic to use against the god who imposed it.
- **Stone Gardens.** Medusas usually live in lairs filled with the statues of previous hunters. The statues are ambush cover, hiding spots to slip between, *and* ammunition — see Shatter Victims in the Malice features, and Stone Puppets in the villain actions, both of which turn the medusa's petrified victims into active combat pieces. The canonical medusa lair is a grotesque garden that's also a loaded weapon.
- **Lifting the Curse.** Agents of the cursing deity can sometimes be persuaded to lift the punishment — usually after the medusa completes an impossible task in the god's name or levels a threat the god can't ignore. Ancient rituals exist too, hidden by wrathful divine servants and usually demanding great sacrifice.
- **Serpentine Reflexes.** Medusas are as fast and nimble as snakes, with Speed 10 and the ability to briefly manifest scaled wings. Don't picture a slow statue-maker picture a deadly close-quarters fighter who also happens to have a petrifying gaze.
- **Baleful Magic.** Beyond petrification, a medusa's kit weakens, charms, and slows — all tools to even the odds when outnumbered.
- **Languages.** Whatever language they spoke before the curse; often Caelian, frequently the tongues of nearby monsters, and occasionally an archaic form of Khamish.

### Director's note on petrification

The book's sidebar is worth relaying: petrification can permanently take a hero out of the game. If you want to preserve the option to get them back, plan in advance for one of: (1) the Find a Cure downtime project in *Draw Steel: Heroes* to recover an ancient ritual; (2) a living medusa who can reverse the state themselves; (3) a nearby temple whose priest can cure petrification — for a price. Pick one before the fight and make sure the party has a thread to pull.

## Shared traits (solo creature)

- **Solo Monster.** *End Effect* — end of each turn, the medusa can take 10 unresistable damage to clear one save-ends effect. *Solo Turns* — two turns per round, never consecutively. Between End Effect and Solo Turns, single-round lockdown strategies against a medusa simply do not work.
- **Cunning Edge.** The medusa has an edge on power rolls against any creature restrained or slowed by Petrify. Petrify is the core of the kit; once a hero is caught in it, everything the medusa throws at them lands harder.
- **Many Peering Eyes.** The medusa can't be flanked. Snakes see in all directions.

## Medusa Malice features

Spent at the start of the medusa's turn.

- **Weakening Glare (4 Malice, Main action, Ranged 10, one creature).** Damage across tiers with weakened EoT at tier 1–2 and weakened save-ends at tier 3. The reliable "soften the front-liner before I petrify them" button — weakened means the target's Might test to resist Petrify is likely to fail.
- **Solo Action (5 Malice).** The medusa takes an additional main action this turn, *even if dazed.* Dazed-proof — every round the Malice economy allows, the medusa can fire Petrify + Damning Gaze + Snake Bites and still have a turn left on the doubled-turn cycle.
- **Ssstop and Lisssten (5 Malice, Main action, Ranged 10, three creatures).** Each target is charmed on a failed Intuition test at a rising difficulty per tier. *At a time of the medusa's choosing,* a charmed creature uses a free triggered action to move up to their speed and make a free strike against an enemy of the medusa's choice, then the charm ends. Friendly-fire button — a charmed defender hits the party wizard from across the room.
- **Shatter Victims (7 Malice, Area, 10-square radius).** Three stone statues within 10 of the medusa each shatter in a 2-cube explosion. Each enemy in one of those areas makes a Might test — *double bane if the enemy is restrained or slowed by Petrify*. Results scale from 6 damage (tier 3) up to 12 damage + vertical push 3 + bleeding save-ends (tier 1). Converts the lair itself into a trio of area blasts focused on already-petrifying heroes.

## Medusa — solo

**Role:** solo
**Tags:** level 5, solo, accursed, humanoid, medusa
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 205

**Flavor:** Size 1M, Stamina 420, Speed 10, Stability 5, Free Strike 8. Agility-first solo. A Level 5 solo stands against about six same-level heroes on their own.

**Tactics:**

**Snake Bites** (Signature, Melee 1, two targets) — heavy damage with slowed save-ends on failed Might tests at rising tier difficulty. Snake venom acts as the melee-range Petrify setup: slowed targets step into the Cunning Edge zone and increase the potency of a follow-up Petrify.

**Damning Gaze** (Signature, Ranged 10, two targets) — damage with push 3/5/7 per tier. **3 Malice:** targets *two additional* creatures or objects for a total of four. Repositioning tool — shove heroes into cover-less squares so Petrify lands harder, or into shatter-able statues for the Malice combo.

**Petrify** (5 Malice, Main action, 3-burst area) — restrained save-ends on Might tests at every tier, with tier 3 also dropping a slowed save-ends floor even on passing saves (and still restrained on M<4). *A target with cover reduces the potency by 1; a slowed target increases the potency by 1* — so Snake Bites + Petrify chains cleanly. Restrained-this-way targets begin magically turning to stone; **a target who ends two consecutive turns still restrained by Petrify is petrified.** The whole fight is a race against Petrify.

**Nimble Escape** (Maneuver, Self) — shift 3 and attempt to hide *even if observed*. Combined with Stone Gardens terrain, the medusa has near-continuous access to hiding, which makes the next turn's ambush strike potentially a double-edge situation.

**Venomous Spit** (Triggered action, Melee 1) — when a creature within reach deals damage to the medusa, acid-damage counterstrike at 13/18/22 per tier. Everyone who closes into melee with the medusa pays a toll in acid.

Villain actions:

- **Mass Petrify (VA1, Magic/Ranged 50, each enemy).** The medusa uses Petrify against *every enemy* without spending Malice. Targets without cover take +1 potency. This is the act-one opener that decides the shape of the fight — party members who took cover survive it; anyone caught in the open is on the Petrify clock.
- **Serpent Wings (VA2, Self).** The medusa manifests scaled wings and flies up to their speed without provoking opportunity attacks. During or after the movement, they use Snake Bites and Damning Gaze *each once*. Maximum relocation and maximum damage in the same villain action — the mid-fight reposition.
- **Stone Puppets (VA3, 10-burst area).** Acid damage with weakened save-ends on failed Presence tests at rising tier. *As a free triggered action, each stone statue and each creature restrained or slowed by Petrify within distance moves up to their speed and uses a signature ability with an edge against an enemy of the medusa's choice.* Statues without their own stats move at speed 5 and use the medusa's free strike. Act three of a medusa fight: the lair itself wakes up, and every hero who was still in Petrify's grip is briefly puppeted into striking their own allies. Design the Stone Garden knowing this VA exists — lots of statues = lots of puppets.

Counterplay: (1) **Resist the Petrify timer at all costs.** Two consecutive turns restrained by Petrify = out of the game. Hand-off save-ends removal between casters if the party has it; if not, consider Solo Action means the medusa can double-Petrify a single round, so keep a clear condition-remover ready. (2) Take cover before the medusa's turn — Mass Petrify and ordinary Petrify both lose potency on targets with cover. (3) Minimize statues in the lair before the fight — Shatter Victims needs three statues within 10, and Stone Puppets rewards every statue on the map. Breaking statues pre-initiative is a valid prep action. (4) End Effect + Solo Turns + Many Peering Eyes means save-or-suck spells and flank bonuses mostly do nothing; bring sustained direct damage. (5) Charmed allies (Ssstop and Lisssten) can be freed early by damaging them — coordinate a small-damage strike that ends the charm before the medusa triggers it.
