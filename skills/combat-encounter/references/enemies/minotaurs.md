# Minotaurs

*Source: Draw Steel Monsters, pp. 206–208. Reference only — stat blocks live in the book.*

Muscular human-bull hybrids with intelligent, sorrowful eyes and a slow-burning temper that — once lit — eats everything in its path. Minotaurs are **Accursed**: the whole lineage traces to the human warrior Thesia "The Bull" Danaria, twisted into the first minotaur by the Beast Lords of Kham as punishment for rebellion. A fellow prisoner, Velathri, befriended rather than fought her; the Beast Lords made *him* a minotaur too; they fell in love; their descendants charged out of the maze and razed Kham. Modern minotaurs prefer labyrinthine terrain — canyons, ruins, dungeons, winding city undercrofts — where the architecture rewards their innate navigation sense and lets them hunt the way their ancestor did. Evil minotaurs exist; so do stoic and noble ones; all of them rage when pushed. And all of them, when they trust someone, trust *absolutely*.

- **Rage as fuel, not weakness.** Minotaurs are dangerous because they close the distance fast and hit hard when they get there. Every non-minion stat block has **Charge** on its signature ability. If the party can't deny a straight-line approach, the minotaur wins round one.
- **Labyrinth natives.** Every minotaur has **Minotaur Sense** — can't score less than a tier-2 outcome on tests to *navigate, search, or seek*. Dungeons, mazes, forests, city sewers: a minotaur GM can't be lost or ambushed on their own ground. Great for GM flavor during chase/pursuit scenes.
- **Deep bonds.** A minotaur who trusts a PC will charge into death for them. Betrayal turns that loyalty into lifelong vendetta. Narrative hook — a minotaur ally is a powerful NPC relationship with high upside and very high downside.
- **The big three.** Most encounters build around one of two **elites** (Minotaur harrier or Sunderer brute) and a swarm of minions (Bullies, Lackeys, or a Stampede). The elites are the brain; the minions are the weight.
- **Languages.** Most minotaurs speak their own dialect of Khamish.

## Shared traits (every minotaur)

- **Minotaur Sense.** Floor of tier-2 on navigate/search/seek tests. Universal and always-on.
- **Accursed + Humanoid.** Keyword tags for damage-type interactions and curse-related item effects.
- **Size 2, slow-to-mid speed.** Every minotaur is Size 2. Minions are Speed 6–8; elites are Speed 6–8 depending on variant; the Stampede is Size 4 and Swarm-tagged.
- **Charge-first signature abilities.** Almost every main-action attack is Charge-typed. An encounter map with clear lines of approach plays into minotaur tactics; one with tight corners and broken sightlines doesn't.

## Minotaur Malice features

Spent at the start of any minotaur's turn.

- **Bull Rush (3 Malice per minotaur).** For each 3 Malice, one minotaur acting this turn gets +4 speed and ignores difficult terrain until start of their next turn. Scalable — spend 6 Malice to buff two minotaurs, 9 for three. Turns a Speed-6 Sunderer into Speed-10 ignoring terrain, making the Charge-signature a cross-map threat.
- **Cut the ... Nonsense! (5 Malice).** One minotaur halves damage taken *and* can Knockback as a free triggered action whenever an enemy comes adjacent until start of their next turn. The "stay out of my face" button — any melee approach triggers a knockback, and whatever damage gets through is halved. Spend before a big party alpha turn.
- **Bullseye (7 Malice).** Every minotaur in the encounter projects psychic impressions of lostness. Each enemy within 5 of a minotaur is teleported up to 5 squares and makes an Intuition test: ≤1 line of effect only within 3 + frightened of all minotaurs save-ends, 2 line of effect only within 3 EoT, 3 no effect. The map-wide perception-lockdown button. A party whose sightlines are capped at 3 squares can't snipe, can't support from range, and is effectively forced into the minotaur's preferred melee range. Pair with Bull Rush — teleport everyone into position, then Charge through what's left.

## Minions

### Minotaur Bully — minion

**Role:** brute
**Tags:** level 8, minion, accursed, humanoid, minotaur
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 208
**With Captain:** +3 damage bonus to strikes.

**Flavor:** Size 2, Stamina 14, Speed 6. Javelin-and-bellow throwing minion.

**Tactics:** **Javelin and Bellow** (Charge, Melee 2 or Ranged 5) — straightforward damage, tier 3 with I<4 applies *taunted EoT or frightened of all minotaurs save-ends*. The choice matters: taunt locks a hero's main action, but frightened (save ends) compounds with Bullseye's save-ends frightened and the elites' fear riders. Four bullies at range 5 can each tag a different party member, splitting the debuff across the whole party. Dual-purpose minion: charge in if adjacent, throw if not.

### Minotaur Lackey — minion

**Role:** harrier
**Tags:** level 8, minion, accursed, humanoid, minotaur
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 208
**With Captain:** +2 bonus to speed.

**Flavor:** Size 2, Stamina 13, Speed 8, Agility +4. Horn-vaulting skirmisher.

**Tactics:** **Horn Vault** (Charge, Melee 2) — damage + slide 1/3 on tier 2/3. **Effect:** a target force-moved into an obstacle with M<3 is *bleeding save-ends*. The whole point of the Lackey is the slide-into-wall combo. On a map with pillars, cliffs, spikes, or chokepoints, four lackeys can charge four heroes into four different obstacles, bleeding each of them. On an open plain this minion barely does anything — map design decides whether the Lackey is strong or weak.

### Minotaur Stampede — minion (swarm)

**Role:** defender
**Tags:** level 10, minion, accursed, humanoid, minotaur, swarm
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 208
**With Captain:** edge on strikes.

**Flavor:** Size 4, Stamina 17, Speed 8. A *swarm* of minotaurs treated as a single minion. Might +5 / Agility +5 — unusually high for a minion.

**Tactics:** **Bull Rush** (Charge, Melee 2) — damage + prone on tier 2, tier 3 adds *can't stand save-ends* on M<5. **Effect on Charge:** each creature the stampede moves *through* with M<4 is knocked prone. So a charging stampede prones every low-Might hero in its path *and* proning-plus-can't-stands the target at the end. **Swarm:** moves through spaces as if Size 2, can occupy other creatures' squares, and makes a free strike against each creature whose space it shares at start of its turns. This is a Size-4 unit that treats the party's formation as empty hallway — drive it straight through the backline, letting it overlap with the healer and controller, free-striking both at the start of each of its turns. Counterplay: stand your ground on high-Might / stability terrain; abilities that kill swarms outright bypass the Stamina 17 chip-damage problem; AoE damage against the whole stampede is the intended answer.

## Elites

### Minotaur — elite

**Role:** harrier
**Tags:** level 3, elite, accursed, humanoid, minotaur
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 207

**Flavor:** Size 2, Stamina 100, Speed 8, Stability 2, Free Strike 5. The base-profile minotaur — agile, charge-focused, flail-and-blade.

**Tactics:** **Flail and Blade** (Charge, Melee 2, two targets) — damage + push 1/2/3 across tiers. **Effect:** minotaur shifts up to 3 after. Charge → hit two heroes → shift out. The canonical hit-and-run: Speed 8 + Charge + push 3 + shift 3 means the minotaur can end its turn 11+ squares from where it started, having hit two targets. **Primal Bay** (3 Malice, Main) — until end of next turn, damage immunity 2 + +5 damage on strikes + *one additional maneuver next turn*. The pre-commit buff: spend Primal Bay on turn one, walk into turn two with an extra maneuver budget for a Charge + Flail + Goring Horns play. **Goring Horns** (5 Malice, Charge, Melee 2, Maneuver) — damage + dazed save-ends on I<0/I<1/I<2; **potency +1 when used as part of the Charge main action.** So a Charge + Flail and Blade + Goring Horns combo (the Primal Bay extra-maneuver payoff) lets one main action become three attacks, one of which dazes. **Retaliatory Strike** (Triggered, when damaged, Ranged 8) — Charge + Flail and Blade or Goring Horns against the attacker. Ranged attacks from within 8 squares eat a full Charge back. Counterplay: strike from beyond 8; burst faster than Primal Bay's damage immunity 2 can absorb.

### Minotaur Sunderer — elite

**Role:** brute
**Tags:** level 3, elite, accursed, humanoid, minotaur
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 207

**Flavor:** Size 2, Stamina 120, Speed 6, Stability 2, Free Strike 6. Heavier, slower, grabs-and-bleeds variant.

**Tactics:** **Spiked Maul** (Charge, Melee 2, two targets) — damage + pull 1/2/3 across tiers. **Effect:** *a target pulled adjacent is automatically grabbed.* Unlike the base minotaur's push-away play, the Sunderer *drags heroes in* and locks them there. **Disemboweling Horns** (3 Malice, Charge, Melee 2, Maneuver) — damage + bleeding save-ends on M<0/M<1/M<2. **Special: the target must be grabbed.** So the pipeline is Spiked Maul → pull + grab → Disemboweling Horns → bleeding. Bleeding rider: 1d6 damage at start of target's turns. A Sunderer with a grabbed hero and Disemboweling Horns' bleed stacked is a ticking kill clock. **Fearsome Bay** (5 Malice, Main, 3-burst) — frightened save-ends on I<0/I<1/I<2 across tiers; until end of next turn, damage immunity 2 + +5 damage on strikes. The Sunderer's Primal Bay equivalent — same durability-plus-damage buff but AoE-frightens instead of extra maneuver. **Retaliatory Strike** (Triggered, Ranged 6) — Charge + Spiked Maul against the attacker. Counterplay: escape-grab maneuvers before Disemboweling lands; high-Might to fail the M< saves; ranged from 7+; break line of sight after damaging.

## Encounter notes

- **No Leader or Solo in this book.** Minotaur encounters scale by mixing elites and minions; there's no centurion-style leader unit for this category. A Sunderer with a Stampede and four Bullies is a mid-tier encounter shape; two base Minotaurs plus eight Lackeys on a cliff-and-spike map is another.
- **Labyrinth terrain is a multiplier, not a garnish.** The stat blocks implicitly assume the GM is building the map for charge lines, wall-slam Horn Vaults, and confusing-the-healer-with-Bullseye scenarios. On a featureless plain these encounters read flatter than their stat blocks suggest.
- **Fear stacks.** Bullseye (Malice), Fearsome Bay (Sunderer), and Javelin and Bellow (Bully) all apply frightened save-ends *of all minotaurs*. A party without fear counters will watch every hero become frightened of every enemy on the board by round two. Bring Presence saves, fear-cleanse, or burst damage that ends the encounter before fear lands.
