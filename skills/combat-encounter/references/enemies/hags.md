# Hags

*Source: Draw Steel Monsters, pp. 168–169. Reference only — stat blocks live in the book.*

When a mortal spellcaster — usually a druid or witch — grows desperate enough to bargain with an evil archfey, they sometimes become a hag: aged, clawed, cruel, and bound to spread their patron's misery across the world. A hag can be ruinously powerful on their own, and the oldest ones have hoarded magic, knowledge, and reputation over centuries.

- **Hiding in Plain Sight.** Hags usually appear as ordinary older humanoids — doting aunts, kindly grandmothers, charitable uncles. The more benevolent the face, the worse the rot beneath. Almost nobody who glimpses a hag's true form survives long enough to describe it.
- **Dealmakers and Heartbreakers.** Hags trade in knowledge, curses, and small magical services — but rarely for coin. They prefer to take something personal: the ability to love, the sound of a child's laugh, a toe. And the deal is almost always subtly worse than advertised. Mortals who get caught up in a fey bargain frequently end up corrupted themselves; a few end up becoming hags.
- **What's in a Name?** Hags name themselves whimsically. The ones who hunt the innocent tend to pick names like Auntie, Uncle, Granny, or Nanny. "Granny Gumdrops" is a red flag.
- **Loyal Underlings.** Hags are treacherous, so they prefer minions that *can't* betray them — animals, constructs, undead, or fey too scared to try.
- **Languages.** Most hags speak Anjali, Caelian, Khelt, and Yllyric. Older hags usually know several more.
- **Hag Goodies.** Hags collect oddities across centuries. Most of a hag's hoard is useless-macabre (a weeping porcelain doll, a stove with living feet, a book that recounts the reader's worst memories), but a handful of items are genuinely useful — sample loot includes the Evilest Eye, Imp's Tongue, Float Powder, and Buzz Balm, plus rare cooking components and project-source notes for specific armor/implement enhancements. Lean into the hoard when placing a hag encounter — the lair is part of the fight.

## Hag Malice features

Spent at the start of the hag's turn.

- **Casting Curses and Bodies (3 Malice).** The hag spits a curse that forces each enemy within 2 squares to be pushed up to 3. Cheap repositioning; break a cluster around the hag to open lines for bigger Malice spends.
- **Hag Wyrd (5 Malice, Main action, 10×1 line).** Fire damage in a long line with a frightened save-ends rider on failed Reason tests across tiers. *After the power roll, the hag can swap damage type and condition to lightning/dazed or cold/slowed.* One Malice spend, three possible configurations — hexer toolkit for the hag.
- **Solo Action (5 Malice).** The hag takes an additional main action on their turn *even if they are dazed*. Dazed-proof — plan for the hag to act twice in a round whenever Malice allows, and to resist single-round lockdown.
- **House Call (10 Malice).** The hag's hut springs up on chicken-leg and enters the map within 10 squares of the hag. Size 4, Stamina 75, damage immunity 3, Speed 8. The hut takes its own turn; its only action is **Kick** (Signature, 2-cube within 2) — big push + prone on a failed Might test at every tier. The "the lair joins the fight" button — drop on round 2–3 when heroes have committed to a position, then pinball the entire party with a moving size-4 kicker.

## Shared traits

- **Supernatural Resistance.** Magic and psionic abilities against a hag take a bane. Plan around this as a hero caster; the hag-specific counterplay is mundane martial pressure, not spell focus.
- **Solo Monster.** *End Effect* — end of each turn, the hag can take 5 unresistable damage to end one save-ends effect. *Solo Turns* — the hag takes two turns per round (never consecutively). Most of the hag's threat density comes from the doubled turn economy; design encounters for a long fight with repeated hero attention rather than a burst check.
- **Fly, hover.** Terrain doesn't reach the hag. Keep line-of-effect matters in mind for party ranged options.

## Wode Hag — solo

**Role:** solo
**Tags:** level 3, solo, fey, hag
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 169

**Flavor:** Size 1L, Stamina 300, Speed 5 with Fly/hover, Stability 1, Free Strike 6. The forest-deep grandmother kind of hag — the cottage, the oven, the explosive pastries, the whole nightmare. A Level 3 solo, so she stands against roughly six same-level heroes on her own.

**Tactics:** **Corrosive Claws** (Signature, Melee 1, two targets) — corruption damage across tiers with weakened save-ends on failed Agility tests. The reliable turn-after-turn action; weakened stacks nicely across the hag's doubled turn economy. **Soul Steal** (Main action, 4-cube within 1) — corruption damage to each enemy in the area, extra damage on failed Presence tests; gains an edge against any target with a soul; **3 Malice upgrade regains Stamina equal to half the damage dealt**. The sustain button — spend 3 Malice when the hag is bloodied. **Shapeshifter** (Maneuver, Self) — assume any size-1 form, from house cat to humanoid; if done outside line of effect, the hag is automatically hidden. **5 Malice upgrade:** becomes size 2 (bear up to ogre) and her melee abilities gain +1 reach and +4 damage. Shapeshift is how a hag starts every encounter that begins with dialogue instead of initiative. **Turned Upside Down** (2 Malice, triggered action, 1-burst) — when an enemy targets the hag with a melee strike, every enemy in the burst is slid 2/3/5 squares (converting to *vertical* slide on failed Reason tests, and adding restrain EoT/save-ends on higher tiers). **A creature vertically force-moved while restrained this way is suspended in midair and falls when the condition ends.** The hag's melee counter — defenders who commit to melee get yeeted into the air and left dangling.

Villain actions across the fight's three acts:
- **Snackies for Sweeties (VA1, 5-burst, area).** The hag attaches an ornate explosive pastry to each creature in the area with a<2. At end of round, she rolls damage against every creature still wearing one. A pastry can be removed as a maneuver with an Agility test — but on a tier 1 result the hag rolls for *all* pastries at once, tier 2 fails to remove, tier 3 permanently disarms. The "mid-fight ticking timer" opener; pairs with the Kick from House Call to send pastry-wearing heroes into walls. Also: the mental image is the entire point of a hag encounter — commit to it.
- **Predator's Alacrity (VA2, 1-burst, area).** The hag shifts up to her speed, uses Corrosive Claws against every enemy in reach, pushes each target 2, then shifts up to her speed again. A mid-fight cleanse-and-reset: land in the cluster, sweep, exit. The doubled turn economy means this villain action can *also* be followed by a second normal turn elsewhere.
- **Open the Oven (VA3, 5-cube within 1).** Fire damage + weakened save-ends on failed Agility tests. *The area becomes a roiling oven until end of encounter; any creature in the oven takes an extra 5 damage from the hag's damage-dealing abilities.* The finisher that rewrites the map into a kill-zone — perfect inside the hut, which is where House Call and the lair notes naturally route heroes anyway.

Counterplay: (1) Supernatural Resistance + doubled turns + End Effect means spell-focus, save-or-suck, and single-round lockdown all underperform — bring sustained martial damage or unavoidable damage. (2) Keep someone within reach of suspended allies who fall from Turned Upside Down; plan for the fall damage. (3) Disarm the pastries before end of round — coordinate which hero takes the Agility test and which provides cover so the tier 1 "hag rolls all pastries" result doesn't land with everyone still wearing one. (4) Don't end a fight inside the oven if VA3 has fired; rotate out of the cube before the hag's next turn.
