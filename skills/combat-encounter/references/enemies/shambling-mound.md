# Shambling Mound

*Source: Draw Steel Monsters, pp. 246–247. Reference only — stat blocks live in the book.*

A wandering mass of rotten vegetation, pungent soil, and writhing vines that drags itself through overgrown swamps, stinking marshes, dense wodes, and dark rainforests, consuming every organic thing it passes. Before most victims realize the ground is moving, they've been engulfed and the shambling mound is already shambling away.

- **Noxious Origins.** Most shambling mounds form where evil or green-magic-wielding mages or hags have been working in secret — latent magic seeps into the surrounding soil and coalesces into a hungry creature. The shambling mound knows only that it must grow, and that to grow it must feed.
- **Regional Toxins.** The mound's poisons change with habitat — coastal mounds can ooze sweet-smelling milky sap like manchineel; rainforest mounds bloom pale angel's-trumpet-style flowers that seduce prey with intoxicating scent and leave them hallucinating. The effect is always the same: sap the victim's strength, then magically absorb it. (See the alchemical-ingredients sidebar below — a slain mound can be harvested for poison-crafting components.)
- **Hearty Eaters.** The mound drags its form through undergrowth, consuming as it goes, but it prefers to settle in a shadowed overgrown place and wait for a meal to walk in. Engulfed prey is sealed into a vegetable sack of interwoven vines and leaves; the mound then leeches strength out of the captives to feast *or* to empower itself.
- **Verdant Core and Expansive Sprawl.** The sack of victims is the obvious part of the mound. The less obvious part is a small magic crux at the heart, sheathed in vines and leaves — the mound can tear that sheath open to unleash vastly more power, but at the cost of making itself vulnerable (see Villain Action 3 below). And the full extent of a shambling mound is hidden among ordinary vegetation; the visible body is only the center of a much wider reach of hostile flora.
- **Keyword: Plant.** The only stat block in the Plant-keyword taxonomy.

### Director's note on alchemical ingredients

The book's sidebar: after a shambling mound dies, a creature can make a Reason test to extract toxins from the remains — tier 1 extracts nothing, tier 2 enough for one vial of poison, tier 3 enough for three vials. Crafting vials from the raw toxins requires the **poison-crafting downtime project** (see *Draw Steel: Heroes*, Chapter 12). Item prerequisite: shambling-mound toxins. Project source: texts or lore in Caelian. Project characteristic: Reason or Intuition. Project goal: 45. A vial of poison applied as a maneuver lets the weapon deal +4 + 1d6 poison damage for one minute. Worth flagging to the party *before* the kill — a savvy group may try to preserve specific mound parts for the Reason check.

## Shared traits (solo creature)

- **Solo Monster.** *End Effect* — end of each turn, the mound can take 10 unresistable damage to clear one save-ends effect. *Solo Turns* — two turns per round, never consecutive. Same template as most solos in this book; plan for a doubled turn economy.
- **Engulfing Sack.** The mound carries engulfed creatures in a vegetable sack with 30 Stamina, damage immunity 5, and *fire weakness 10*. Destroying the sack frees all trapped creatures. The sack **regrows at the start of the mound's next turn.** So heroes can rescue a captive — but only for one round unless they also kill the mound before it regrows the sack.
- **False Appearance.** While motionless, the shambling mound is indistinguishable from ordinary vegetation. The encounter starts with the party standing on it.
- **Frothing Flora.** The area within 6 squares of the mound is difficult terrain. The mound always fights on terrain that punishes the party's movement.

## Shambling Mound Malice features

Spent at the start of the mound's turn.

- **Poisoned Vines (3 Malice, Maneuver).** The next Vine Lash the mound uses before end of its next turn deals +12 poison damage to each target. Cheap damage rider — spend before committing to a big Vine Lash round.
- **Frenzy Lash (5 Malice).** The mound lashes out at each enemy within 10 squares. Agility test — tier 1 is 7 damage + push 7 or vertical push 3 + restrained save-ends, tier 2 is 6 damage + push 5 or vertical push 2, tier 3 is 3 damage. Map-wide push + restraint; combos brutally with Frothing Flora difficult terrain and with the mound's Tether Down reaction.
- **Solo Action (5 Malice).** The mound takes an additional main action this turn, *even if dazed.* Dazed-proof action economy — the mound can fire a full Vine Lash + Engulf combination in a single round whenever Malice allows.
- **Leeching Wilds (7 Malice).** Until end of next turn, the area within 10 squares is difficult terrain for enemies and each enemy takes a bane on power rolls. *Any enemy who starts their turn in the area takes 4 acid damage, and the shambling mound regains an equal amount of Stamina.* The self-heal + whole-party debuff button — spend when the fight has reached half-HP and threatens to tip against the mound.

## Shambling Mound — solo

**Role:** solo
**Tags:** level 5, solo, plant, shambling mound
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 247

**Flavor:** Size 3, Stamina 400, Speed 3, Stability 5, Free Strike 7. Mightily strong (+4), slow, agility-negative. Speed 3 is a feature — the shambling mound doesn't move much, it makes you come to *it*.

**Tactics:**

**Vine Lash** (Signature, Melee 6, two targets) — heavy damage with grabbed at tier 1 on A<3, tier 2 on A<4, tier 3 auto-grab. Six squares of melee reach means the mound threatens most of a standard battlefield without moving. **2 Malice:** slide each target up to 6 — pull heroes into the engulf zone. **3 Malice:** each target takes +7 poison.

**Seismic Slam** (Main action, 6-burst area) — damage with dazed save-ends on failed Might tests escalating per tier. The 6-burst is wider than most heroes can safely retreat from in one turn, given Frothing Flora converts the area to difficult terrain. A Solo Action Malice round lets the mound do Vine Lash + Seismic Slam in one turn.

**Engulf** (2 Malice, Main action, Melee 6) — one size 1L or smaller target; on failed Agility test (A<3), the target is sealed into the mound's sack. *If the target was already grabbed by the mound, the potency increases by 1* — so Vine Lash grab → Engulf is the canonical combo. Engulfed creatures are restrained, take 3 poison damage at start of each turn, can't take damage from abilities used from *outside* the sack, and move with the mound. If the mound dies or the sack is destroyed, engulfed creatures appear in an unoccupied space within 2 of where the mound was. **2+ Malice upgrade:** one extra target per 2 additional Malice. So 6 Malice engulfs up to three heroes in a single action.

**Leech** (Maneuver, Self) — each engulfed creature takes 5 poison; the mound gains 5 temp Stamina per engulfed creature who takes damage. Free sustain every turn the mound has captives — and with the doubled-turn economy, Leech can fire twice per round.

**Tether Down** (Triggered action, Melee 6) — when a creature within 6 willingly moves, damage with restrained EoT on failed Might tests across tiers. The mound punishes movement — disengaging from the melee zone costs a restrain save. Combined with Frothing Flora (already difficult terrain) and Leeching Wilds Malice (banes on power rolls), the whole area around the mound becomes a movement trap.

Villain actions:

- **Ravenous Overgrowth (VA1, 10×2 line).** Damage + pull 3/4/6 per tier, plus *poison weakness 3 at tier 2, poison weakness 5 at tier 3, both until end of encounter.* Pulls heroes closer to the mound *and* stacks poison weakness so Poisoned Vines Malice and Leech and the passive 3-poison-per-turn-while-engulfed all hit harder for the rest of the fight.
- **Composting (VA2, Melee 6, each enemy).** The mound uses Engulf against every enemy in reach *without spending Malice.* Potentially mass-engulfs the entire party in a single action. If Vine Lash has already grabbed anyone, Composting's potency is boosted on them.
- **Exposed Crux (VA3, Self).** The mound rips itself apart, exposing the crux of magic at its core. The mound's melee distance increases to 10, the mound has a double edge on power rolls, *and strikes made against the mound gain an edge.* A mutual escalation — the mound hits harder and with more reach, but every hero strike against it is at edge too. Act-three finale when both sides are ready to trade hits.

Counterplay: (1) **Fire damage.** The Engulfing Sack has fire weakness 10; any engulfed ally is best recovered with fire damage shredding the sack (just don't hit your own engulfed ally with the splash). (2) **Don't move next to the mound.** Tether Down triggers on *willing* movement, so shifts are safer than moves — and teleport-style effects bypass the reaction entirely. (3) **Kill the mound before the sack regrows.** The sack regrows at start of mound's next turn, so the window to free captives is short — coordinate a burst round that kills both the sack and the mound. (4) **Stay out of the 6-square Frothing Flora radius** for ranged characters whenever possible, and particularly out of the 10-square Leeching Wilds zone once that Malice fires. (5) **Poison weakness compounds.** Kill the mound before Ravenous Overgrowth VA1 lands, or plan around permanent poison-weakness for the rest of the fight.
