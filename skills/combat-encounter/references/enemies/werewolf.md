# Werewolf

*Source: Draw Steel Monsters, pp. 322–323. Reference only — stat block lives in the book.*

Shapeshifters filled with the need to rend and tear. Werewolves used to be ordinary humans, elves, polders, or other humanoids, until a feral compulsion took over — eyes blood red, body swelling into a massive wolf or an abominable hybrid. The horror of the encounter is as much in what the werewolf *was* as what they've become.

- **Howling Primordial Chaos.** Werewolves conduct raw Primordial Chaos; their bodies shift into beastlike shapes to contain the overflow. A single bite passes the curse to a new host.
- **Brokered or Cursed.** Two origin paths. Evil nobles actively *seek out* the curse via devil-pacts to extend their power and instill fear, usually in exchange for surrendering their legacy at death. Ordinary folk don't have that luxury or the councils to help temper the beast, so small communities tend to oppress their own accursed kin out of terror.
- **On Wights and Weres.** The same primordial powers that created werewolves are also harnessed by the stormwight fury tradition. Werewolves subconsciously treat stormwight-fury heroes as rivals and sport. The book notes a **Shared Ferocity** sidebar: the Director gains bonus Malice the first time a creature uses a ferocity ability in the werewolf's line of effect. Plan stormwight-fury heroes at the table accordingly.
- **Languages.** Whatever the werewolf spoke before the curse, plus possibly Khamish, Anjali, or Khelt from researching their condition.

### Director's note on lycanthropy

The werewolf's bite can afflict a hero with **lycanthropy** — a permanent curse removed only by the **Find a Cure** downtime project in *Draw Steel: Heroes*. Afflicted heroes accumulate rage in combat in a way that doesn't clear on respite, compounding with the werewolf's Accursed Rage mechanic below. Flag this to the table in advance: a bitten hero is dealing with the consequences across multiple sessions. Decide whether lycanthropy becomes a *character hook* (the player explores and possibly embraces the curse) or a *ticking clock* (the party races Find a Cure).

## Shared traits (solo creature)

- **Solo Monster.** End Effect lets the werewolf shed save-ends conditions at a cost each turn; Solo Turns gives them two non-consecutive turns per round. Plan for a doubled turn economy and assume single-round lockdown is impossible.
- **Accursed Rage.** The werewolf's core mechanic. Abilities inflict *rage points* on enemies (any enemy except a stormwight fury). When a hero starts their turn with 10+ rage, they expend the rage and are forced to shift toward the nearest creature and make a melee free strike — *potentially against an ally*. Rage resets after a respite for normal heroes, but a lycanthropic hero's rage doesn't clear. The fight is fundamentally about managing rage accumulation, not just Stamina loss.
- **Shapeshifter.** The werewolf enters combat in hybrid humanoid form and can't be shape-changed by external effects. Polymorph-style control doesn't work.
- **Vukenstep.** Ignores difficult terrain.

## Werewolf Malice features

Spent at the start of the werewolf's turn.

- **Blood In Their Eyes** (cheapest). Short-duration speed and temporary Stamina boost — *but can't be used if the werewolf took any holy damage since their last turn.* Holy-damage hitters suppress this feature on the following round.
- **Solo Action.** Extra main action this turn, usable even while dazed. Dazed-proof turn economy.
- **Moonfall** (most expensive). Late-fight escalator: the map turns to night under an impossibly huge moon. While the werewolf has line of effect to the moon, they gain an extra move action or maneuver per turn, and any hero with rage accumulates more rage each round they also have line of effect to the moon. Counterplay is terrain — move under a roof, into a cave, or behind a cloud.

## Werewolf — solo

**Role:** solo
**Tags:** level 1, solo, accursed, humanoid, werebeast
**Source:** Draw Steel Monsters, p. 323

**Flavor:** Size 1M, high Speed, Might/Agility-focused. A Level 1 Solo — stands against about six same-level heroes alone. The fight's threat curve is less about hero Stamina and more about how many rage points the party is carrying by round three.

**Tactics:**

**Accursed Bite** is the rage-building Signature — a Charge-typed melee strike that damages and adds a meaningful chunk of rage to the target. Spending Malice on it can afflict the target with lycanthropy on a failed Presence test, and the lycanthropy potency increases on repeated bites against the same target who keeps resisting. Heroes with strong Presence may shrug the first bite off and still succumb to the third.

**Ripping Claws** is a two-target melee strike with bleeding and rage riders. Fast-and-dirty melee option when the werewolf isn't leaning on a Charge.

**Berserker Slash** is a short-range burst — the werewolf shifts before using it, so it lands wherever the most heroes are clumped.

**Wall Leap** (maneuver) is acrobatic traversal — jumps to a wall, then jumps off the wall with a free strike and potential prone. Encounter map terrain matters; ruined towers, alley walls, or sewer corridors all give the werewolf a third dimension.

**Facepalm and Head Slam** (triggered action) punishes heroes who charge or move in a straight line toward the werewolf for a melee strike. The target is knocked prone and takes damage *before* their own strike resolves. Shifting into melee position avoids the trigger; charging feeds it.

Villain actions:

- **Howl (VA1).** Burst around the werewolf. Each enemy makes an Intuition test, with frighten and forced-retreat consequences on failure. The important rider: any enemy with even a single rage point gains a significant pile more rage and "howls along" with the werewolf — so the party cascades toward the 10-rage free-strike threshold.
- **Full Wolf (VA2).** Mid-fight phase change — the werewolf transforms into a size-3 wolf for the rest of the encounter, gaining substantial speed, extra damage, an extra rage-application per hit, and an increased lycanthropy potency on the bite. Reserve for when the party is feeling comfortable.
- **Rampage (VA3).** Large rage-applying area attack with a shift before and after. The finale — typically crosses the last few party members over the 10-rage threshold and sets up cascading friendly-fire on the following round.

Counterplay: (1) **Holy damage denies Blood In Their Eyes** — every round with a holy strike costs the werewolf their cheapest Malice feature. Cleric/paladin relevance is baked in. (2) **Rage is the real win condition, not Stamina.** Track rage openly; a hero at full Stamina with 12 rage is in worse shape than one at 0 Stamina and stabilized, because the rage-compelled free strike can break the encounter. (3) **Ranged kiting helps, charging hurts.** Most of the werewolf's kit is melee-adjacent, so ranged parties with cover can starve rage opportunities — but straight-line charging toward the werewolf triggers Facepalm and Head Slam. Shift into position when the option exists. (4) **Deny the moon if Malice climbs to the Moonfall threshold.** Move the fight inside, underground, or use cloud/smoke effects. (5) **Killing the werewolf doesn't cure a bitten hero** — plan Find a Cure as a follow-up arc. (6) **Stormwight fury heroes are rage-immune but fuel Director Malice** via Shared Ferocity — pace their ferocity spends carefully.
